# Two independent simulated beta sessions

Date: 2026-09-08

Tested the current local development build, including the money/prestige display and targeted development, rather than the older public deployment. Two AI personas chose their own actions from a cold start on separate fresh browser saves. Their own browser surfaces were unavailable, so the main agent operated the UI and relayed visible screens and actual outcomes. Neither tester read the source, design documents, or the other tester's reactions. This is qualitative, brokered AI testing, not human usability research or a measured mobile-session study.

Both sessions completed the 12-week regular season, six postseason/offseason steps, and entry into 2028. Browser error logs returned no errors. Final desktop screenshots were emitted in the conversation, not saved as files. No game code was changed during this review.

## Different approaches and outcomes

| | College-football diehard | General gamer, new to football |
|---|---|---|
| First bet | Caleb Banks: developing starting quarterback with three seasons remaining | Owen Hill: strongest-looking initial prospect, high researched potential, three seasons remaining |
| Recruitment | Signed Caleb; later won Micah Ward against Fieldhouse with a lower commission and dedicated support | Won Owen against Northstar with a lower commission and dedicated support |
| Career choices | Trained Caleb's accuracy and Micah's pursuit; returned Caleb to school; prepared final-year Micah for the draft | Delivered Owen's income promise, trained vision, accepted a performance-triggered sponsor, returned him to school |
| Standout moment | Caleb's one-point first win, training improvement, weekly award, and sponsor inquiry | First campaign fulfilled Owen's ambition and earned a referral; later performance opened a sponsor and prestige tier |
| Year-two position | $90,863 cash, 22 prestige; Caleb retained. Micah drafted in Round 7 but missed a roster and had no ongoing contract | $67,856 cash, 18 prestige; Owen retained with ability 77.8 (from 75), profile 43 (from 21), and 98 trust |

These are two selected strategies and random outcomes, not a controlled economy comparison. Training gains were explicit; later game results cannot be attributed solely to training.

**Transfer coverage correction:** Neither persona exercised a school move. Both returning clients stayed with their current teams, and testing stopped on entry into the next preseason. The quiet-offseason finding therefore describes that path, not the value of a transfer market. The current school comparison opens in preseason and previews role, snaps, and new sponsor value, but completes an eligible move immediately for $2,500 rather than resolving competing school offers. Meaningful offseason school and NIL decisions should be surfaced and tested before using these sessions to justify compressing the offseason. Any advance control should skip only settled periods and stop at relevant decisions.

## Changes most strongly supported by these sessions

1. **Make clients remember the support they received.** The same request to invest in their game repeated after coaching and training. Both testers disengaged from those conversations. Reference recent work, current priorities, and individual reactions; avoid repeated requests with unchanged context.
2. **Compress periods with nothing to decide.** The newcomer's returning-only roster passed through six offseason steps, losing $9,000 to overhead with little relevant activity. Offer a clear advance-to-next-relevant-event route with cost and pending-decision visibility.
3. **Make the discovery-to-payoff chain easier to follow.** Preserve scouting reveals, targeted improvements, named rivals, performance-triggered offers, and unlocks. Explain failed training with an actionable next option and explain performance-point thresholds where they affect goals.
4. **Show a credible financial path.** Both could understand individual deal margins, but the newcomer met every promise and still lost roughly one-third of starting capital. Surface season costs, client take-home, and near-term earning options. At draft preparation, connect research and uncertain likely outcomes to the spending decision. These runs do not establish the correct balance values.
5. **Give prospects and career endings more identity.** Initial cards felt numerically similar; the newcomer lacked a plain-language reason to care about positions and schools. The diehard wanted more distinctive football stories. Micah's draft-to-roster-cut ending also needs a clear account of what happened and whether any further agency action is available.

Keep the persistent money and prestige goals. The clearest source of engagement in both sessions was choosing someone to believe in and seeing that choice produce opportunities, rewards, or a meaningful setback.
