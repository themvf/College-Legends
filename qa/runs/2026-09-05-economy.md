**Observation.** The *median* LOW program has lost 63% of its opening reserve in
five seasons and is burning $76K–$108K a week. Seven have crossed zero and the
count is roughly doubling each season. Nine touched zero at some point.

**This is not the intended slow drift.** `economy.ts` documents the intent
precisely: *"at roughly six weeks of turnover the same drift takes about twenty
seasons"*. Measured, the LOW median holds $2.11M against a $986K weekly turnover
by 2031 — **about two weeks of operating cash, not six**, and it took five
seasons to get there, not twenty. The commit that set `OPENING_RESERVE` measured
2–3 of 72 insolvent by season five; I measure 7.

**Diagnosis (hypothesis).** Two things separate this run from the one that
measured 3:

1. **Scale-dependence of postseason cash, which is large.** At 24 programs a LOW
   program reaches the 12-team playoff often; at 72 it almost never does.
   Postseason bonus cash over the whole run, per tier:

   | run | LOW bonus cash | MID | POWER |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | 24 programs, 20 seasons | **$62.60M** | $193.10M | $98.30M |
   | 72 programs, 5 seasons | **$2.20M** | $6.70M | $86.60M |

   Normalised per program-season that is $313K at 24 programs against $15K at
   72 — a **21× difference in the cash a LOW program receives**, and it is the
   difference between the LOW median growing 5.9× (24 programs, 20 seasons) and
   collapsing to 0.37× (72 programs, 5 seasons). The 12-team playoff is a fixed
   number of berths shared over a variable number of programs, so the per-program
   subsidy scales as `1/N`. **Any figure in the economy tuned at 24 programs does
   not transfer to 72.**

2. **The rival planner is still building facilities it cannot carry** — see C2.
   LOW facility upkeep is 20.1% → 21.9% of expenses over the five seasons while
   the tier is losing money every week.

**Confirmation seed.** `qa-c3-econ-72b`, 72 programs, 5 seasons, n = 360.
Independent league, same scale:

| season | insolvent, seed A | insolvent, seed B | ever negative, B | LOW median, B |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2027 | 0/72 | 0/72 | 0 | $5.55M |
| 2028 | 0/72 | 0/72 | 0 | $4.37M |
| 2029 | 1/72 | **2**/72 | 2 | $3.08M |
| 2030 | 4/72 | **3**/72 | 5 | $1.89M |

**Both seeds land on 7 of 72 by season five.** Seed B is slightly worse

fallen to $994K — **0.18× its opening reserve, and about one week of its own
$1.04M weekly turnover.** This is not seed noise.

**Why I am calling this P1 rather than "the LOW drift is intended".** The
charter and the brief both exclude *a* LOW program drifting slowly negative.
This is not that. It is the *median* LOW program losing two thirds of its
reserve in five seasons, an insolvency count more than double the stated figure,
and a curve whose second derivative is positive. On this trajectory the bottom
30 of the league is gone inside one nine-year career, which is the failure the
`OPENING_RESERVE` work was done to prevent.

## C2 — The rival planner posts an expense figure it never pays · **P2**

`packages/ai/src/index.ts`, lines 235 and 326, both identical:

```ts
weeklyExpenses: operatingCost(program, stadiumCapacity(program.facilities.STADIUM), mediaRights(program).total).total,
```

The third argument of `operatingCost` is `weeklyRevenue`. The engine passes the
program's **total** revenue (`index.ts:5231`):

```ts
const revenue = media.total + ticketRevenue + concessionRevenue + sponsorPayment.total;
const operating = operatingCost(program, capacity, revenue);
```

The planner passes **media rights alone**. Since `operations = revenue × 0.55`
and media is a minority of revenue, the planner's own posted cost of running
itself is systematically low.

Measured, seeds `qa-c3-econ-a` + `qa-c3-econ-b`, 24 programs, 8 weeks,
n = 384 program-weeks. Shortfall = charged − posted:

| split | tier | n | median posted | median charged | median shortfall | mean | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| road week | LOW | 92 | $583K | $621K | $34K | $31K | $99K |
| road week | MID | 86 | $1.01M | $1.09M | $70K | $75K | $174K |
| road week | POWER | 38 | $1.70M | $1.85M | $200K | $177K | $278K |
| **home week** | **LOW** | 68 | $587K | **$1.59M** | **$994K** | $1.00M | $1.26M |
| **home week** | **MID** | 74 | $1.02M | **$3.06M** | **$2.01M** | $2.06M | $2.80M |
| **home week** | **POWER** | 26 | $1.70M | **$5.33M** | **$3.63M** | $3.65M | $4.13M |

**Exact agreement: 0 of 384. The posted figure understates in 384 of 384.** On a
POWER home week the planner believes running the program costs $1.70M when the
engine charges it $5.33M — it is out by a factor of 3.1. Independently
reproduced at 72 programs (`qa-c3-econ-72`, n = 432): 0 of 432 exact.

The error is bimodal because the gate only pays at home, so on a road week
revenue really is close to media + sponsorship and the shortfall is small. It is
the home weeks — where the money is — that the planner cannot see.

**Blast radius, measured rather than assumed.** Two gates consume this number:

```
ai/src/index.ts:642  if (cost === undefined || view.budget < cost + view.weeklyExpenses * 2) return [];   // facility build
ai/src/index.ts:951  if (view.availableBudget < candidate.signingCost + post.buyout + view.weeklyExpenses * 2) continue;   // hire
```

I re-ran `selectFacilityUpgrade` for every program on three seeds with the
figure corrected to realised revenue. **0 of 48 decisions flipped** at 24
programs and **0 of 7 week-1 builds** at 72. The shortfall is doubled by the
`× 2`, but `cost` is $350K–$3M and the reserve is $6.5M–$20M, so the boundary is
not usually near.

**So why P2 and not P3.** Three reasons, and the lead should weigh them:

- It is exactly the defect class the charter names — *"the finance panel once
  recomputed operating cost from an approximation and understated it"* — and it
  has recurred, in the AI rather than the panel, with the same sign.
- It is the input to the *specific* rule that the `22 → 3` insolvency fix rests
  on. That rule exists to stop a rival converting reserves into permanent cost.
  It is currently making that judgement on a cost figure that is 3× too low on
  the weeks that matter, and C1 says the insolvency count has drifted back up.
- `weeklyExpenses` is a persisted field of a `DecisionKnowledgeSnapshot`
  (`coachingPlanningKnowledgeSnapshot`), so a wrong number is being written into
  the attributed-decision record as evidence of why the AI chose what it chose.

The zero flip count is the honest mitigation and I am reporting it as loudly as
the defect. This is a correctness defect with no measured behavioural
consequence at these seeds — one balance change away from having one.

the league survives, and it is invisible to the event log.

1. The player's inbox and any finance history cannot show where the money came

2. `analytics.summarize()` and any future finance panel cannot reconstruct a

   `lastWeeklyNet` P1 in cycle 1.** Anything that ever derives a figure by
   summing the event log will silently miss $750K × 12 × seasons. Nothing does
   today; `lastWeeklyNet` used to, and that is precisely how it went wrong.

The fix is a field on the existing events plus one new event for the berth, not
a mechanism change.

### The sink is sized correctly; nobody uses it

`weeklyDonorCapacity` is the ceiling. Seed `qa-c3-econ-a`, 24 programs, per-tier
medians (n = 10 / 10 / 4):

| season end | tier | donor capacity/wk | committed/wk | **% of the ceiling used** | fanBase |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2027 | LOW | $21K | $3K | 12.7% | 27,555 |
| 2027 | MID | $61K | $6K | 10.3% | 64,690 |
| 2027 | POWER | $197K | $4K | **2.2%** | 131,563 |
| 2030 | LOW | $27K | $8K | 27.5% | 33,945 |
| 2030 | MID | $115K | $20K | 17.6% | 104,472 |
| 2030 | POWER | **$592K** | **$11K** | **1.9%** | 357,749 |

POWER's donor ceiling triples in four seasons ($197K → $592K) while its actual
NIL spend stays flat at $11K. **The faucet the design calls "THE money sink"
drains 1.9% of what it is plumbed for at the tier that needs draining, and 27.5%
at the tier that cannot afford it.** The drain is regressive.

**Diagnosis (hypothesis).** Two structural caps in the rival planner
(`ai/src/index.ts:764–773`), not in the NIL model:

```ts
for (const prospect of discovered.slice(0, 2)) {      // at most two prospects a week
  const desired = Math.min(Math.round(uncommittedCapacity / nilOpenings / 50) * 50, ask);
```

- **`slice(0, 2)`** caps a program at two NIL offers a week whatever it can afford.
- **`Math.min(..., ask)`** caps every offer at the market asking price. `ask` is
  `NIL_BASE_PRICE (400) × hypeCurve × positionPremium`, roughly $2.4K/week for an
  80-hype prospect. Two of those is ~$5K a week against a $592K ceiling.

So a rival's NIL spend is bounded by the *price of players*, not by its wealth,
and the price of players does not grow with the league's money. `nil.ts`'s own
docstring says money should be *"a tiebreaker"* and the offer curve is designed
so *"paying his ask buys ~63% of the ceiling, double ~86%, quadruple ~98%"* —
the curve explicitly anticipates offers at 4× ask, and the planner never makes
one. The design intent that *"a low-prestige program can outbid a blueblood"*
cannot be exercised by any rival.

Both are within the variance I would expect from a single seed at n = 4 POWER

## C5 — Winning versus losing: the invariant fails, the mechanism does not · P3, read the caveat

I ran the charter's test three ways because the literal form is confounded over
a 20-season horizon.

### (a) The charter's literal test — pooled, per season

n = 960 program-seasons (both long seeds).

| season | worst winning (≥9W) | best losing (≥9L) | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2027 | $4.50M (MID 9-5) | $3.36M (LOW 2-10) | pass |
| 2028 | $4.26M (MID 9-5) | $4.35M (LOW 3-9) | **FAIL** |
| 2029 | $3.34M (MID 10-4) | $4.98M (LOW 1-11) | **FAIL** |
| 2030 | $3.71M (MID 9-5) | $2.10M (LOW 2-10) | pass |
| … | | | |
| 2041 | $823K (LOW 9-5) | $18.66M (POWER 3-9) | **FAIL** |
| 2043 | **−$863K** (LOW 10-3) | $11.71M (POWER 3-9) | **FAIL** |
| 2044 | $1.64M (LOW 10-4) | $28.54M (MID 3-9) | **FAIL** |
| 2046 | $6.11M (LOW 9-5) | $12.14M (MID 3-9) | **FAIL** |

**17 of 20 seasons fail.** Pooled: winning n = 83, median $7.27M; losing n = 90,
median −$1.03M (at 72 programs). Within tier: LOW fails 8 of 16, MID 8 of 11,
POWER 0 of 8. Within a tier *and* a 4-season era — controlling for both size and
calendar — **10 of 11 tier-eras fail.**

### (b) The mechanism, which passes cleanly

Operating margin (`WEEKLY_FINANCES` net ÷ revenue) by wins, n = 960, pooled:

| wins | n | median margin | median budget change ÷ revenue |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 7 | 5.7% | 1.3% |
| 2 | 47 | 8.4% | 6.2% |
| 4 | 118 | 9.4% | 6.8% |
| 6 | 145 | 10.9% | 8.2% |
| 8 | 95 | 13.9% | 14.0% |
| 10 | 52 | 15.6% | 16.0% |
| 12 | 26 | 15.1% | 19.2% |
| 14 | 10 | 19.7% | 24.3% |
| 15 | 4 | 20.5% | 27.4% |

**Monotone at essentially every step.** Winning pays more than losing *per dollar
of revenue*, which is the invariant `OPERATING_SHARE` was designed to guarantee
and the committed test asserts. At 72 programs the absolute version is clean too:
median change by wins runs −$1.33M at 0 wins to +$13.64M at 15, crossing zero
between 5 and 6 wins.

### (c) So what is failing

The absolute min-vs-max test compares programs of wildly different size. The ten
most profitable losing seasons are all large programs — a MID program going 3-9
in 2044 on **$107.79M** of revenue banks $28.54M at a 26.5% margin. The ten least
profitable winning seasons are all LOW programs, and **every one of them spent
$1.5M–$3.0M on a facility** in the season measured, while every one of the top
losing seasons spent **$0**. Winning programs buy things; the money went
somewhere, which is a sink working.

**The caveat I want the lead to read.** I am calling this P3 because the causal
mechanism the charter is guarding against — costs scaling superlinearly with
standing while revenue scales linearly — is demonstrably **not** present, and the
margin ladder proves it. But the charter says a failure here is a P1 at any
sample size and admits no pooling argument, and I have a genuine failure of the
stated test in 10 of 11 size-and-calendar-controlled cells. I do not think the
economy is broken; I think **the invariant as written is not testable at 20
seasons**, because by then revenue dispersion within a tier is 3.5× and swamps
the record. If the lead wants this invariant to keep meaning something, it needs
restating as a margin or revenue-normalised test. I would rather raise that than
quietly pass it.

Also worth recording: the season-1 bands in `game-balance.md` §3 no longer hold.

| | documented | measured, season 1, 24 programs, both seeds |
|---|---|---|
| winning season (≥9W) | +$1.3M … +$12.0M | +$4.50M … +$16.36M / +$4.81M … +$16.21M |
| losing season (≥9L) | −$0.6M … −$1.8M | **+$157K … +$1.92M / −$505K … +$3.36M** |

**A losing season is now profitable at 24 programs**, where the doc says it costs
$0.6M–$1.8M. It is still a loss at 72 (median −$1.03M), so this is the same
`1/N` postseason-cash scale-dependence as C1 — the doc's figures were taken at 24
programs, and that is the wrong scale to have taken them at.

## C6 — `operations` at 59%–70% of expenses · P3, observation

The charter names 60%+ as the smell that localised the runaway-in-reverse, so I
am reporting it, with the reason I do not think it is that fault.

Expense composition, share of charged expenses, seed `qa-c3-econ-a`, 24 programs
(n = 10 / 10 / 4):

| season | tier | squad | facilities | stadium | **operations** | payroll | NIL | advertising |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2027 | LOW | 11.7% | 17.3% | 12.2% | 55.6% | 3.0% | 0.1% | 0.0% |
| 2027 | MID | 6.2% | 19.5% | 8.9% | **63.1%** | 2.2% | 0.2% | 0.0% |
| 2027 | POWER | 3.7% | 20.7% | 7.6% | **65.8%** | 2.1% | 0.1% | 0.0% |
| 2036 | LOW | 6.2% | 22.7% | 7.6% | **60.6%** | 2.1% | 0.8% | 0.0% |
| 2036 | MID | 3.8% | 24.8% | 6.6% | **61.5%** | 2.8% | 0.6% | 0.0% |
| 2036 | POWER | 2.7% | 19.7% | 5.6% | **69.8%** | 1.8% | 0.3% | 0.0% |
| 2046 | LOW | 3.9% | 27.0% | 7.6% | 59.3% | 1.6% | 0.6% | 0.0% |
| 2046 | MID | 2.8% | 20.6% | 5.9% | **68.0%** | 2.1% | 0.4% | 0.0% |
| 2046 | POWER | 2.8% | 20.7% | 5.9% | **69.3%** | 1.1% | 0.2% | 0.0% |

MID and POWER are over 60% in every one of the twenty seasons. POWER peaks at
70.2% (2039).

**Diagnosis.** This is structural and cannot be otherwise given
`OPERATING_SHARE = 0.55`. Operations is 55% of revenue by definition; every
other cost is bounded (`squad = scholarshipLimit × $1,500`,
`stadium = capacity × $3`, `facilities = Σ level^1.7 × $12,000`, all with hard
ceilings), while revenue is not. So as a program grows, operations' share of
expenses rises monotonically toward 100% and **total expenses asymptote to 55%
of revenue — a 45% margin ceiling**. Observed margins are 15%–20% and still
climbing, so the compounding is not finished.

I flag this not as the runaway-in-reverse — the sign is opposite and the margin
ladder in C5(b) is healthy — but because the charter's own 60% heuristic can now
never *not* fire, so it has stopped being able to detect anything. That is worth
the lead knowing about the heuristic itself.

**A second-order note:** squad cost falls from 11.7% to 3.9% of LOW expenses over
twenty seasons. `SQUAD_COST_PER_SCHOLARSHIP` is a fixed $1,500 against a
scholarship limit that does not move, so the roster becomes economically free.

## C7 — Reserves: failure mode 3 passes, with a residue · P3

Seed `qa-c3-econ-72`, identical league and seed, every opening balance ×3.

| | baseline | reserve ×3 |
|---|---|---|
| insolvent by season 5 | **7/72** | **0/72** |
| went negative at any point, 2031 | 9 | 0 |
| LOW median budget 2031 | $2.11M (0.37× of opening) | $9.88M |

**PASS.** Insolvency improves, so nothing is converting reserves wholesale into
recurring cost. The build-discipline rule is doing its job.

**The residue.** LOW weekly net is *worse* at every season with the larger
reserve, and LOW facility upkeep is a larger share of expenses:

| season | LOW weekly net, baseline | LOW weekly net, ×3 | LOW facilities % of expenses, baseline | ×3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2027 | −$91K | **−$117K** | 20.1% | 22.2% |
| 2028 | −$101K | **−$128K** | 21.0% | 23.8% |
| 2029 | −$108K | **−$141K** | 21.7% | 25.2% |
| 2030 | −$105K | **−$155K** | 22.2% | 25.8% |
| 2031 | −$76K | **−$118K** | 21.9% | 25.7% |

So roughly **$40K a week of every marginal reserve dollar still becomes permanent
cost** — the `deeplyReserved` branch (`budget >= cost + upkeep × 400`) is doing
exactly what it is designed to, and a program holding 3× reserves builds more.
That is intended behaviour, and it stays intended right up until the extra
upkeep exceeds what the extra reserve buys. It does not here. But it means the
mechanism the last collapse ran on is still live, merely correctly gated, and it
is worth a committed regression test at 72 programs rather than a one-off check.

One more thing the reserve run shows: with ×3 reserves the winning-vs-losing test
gets **worse** (worst winning season −$3.38M), because the extra money is spent
on facilities by exactly the programs that are winning. Same mechanism as C5(c).

## C8 — The unbounded quantity now drives revenue · P3, design observation

**Failure mode 2 passes on cost.** ×30 `fanBase`, seed `qa-c3-econ-a`, 24
programs:

| line | moved | mean ratio |
|---|---|---|
| squad | 0 / 24 | 1.000 |
| facilities | 0 / 24 | 1.000 |
| stadium | 0 / 24 | 1.000 |
| payroll | 0 / 24 | 1.000 |
| NIL | 0 / 24 | 1.000 |
| media | 0 / 24 | 1.000 |
| gate | 12 / 24 | 1.271 |
| operations | 12 / 24 | 1.231 |

**Cost base moved in 0 of 24.** Operations moves only as a share of revenue, and
revenue moved only 23% on a 3,000% fan increase because the gate clamps at
capacity — exactly the bounded behaviour the charter asks for.

**But `fanBase` still has no ceiling, and sponsorship is linear in it across
seasons.** Seed `qa-c3-econ-a`, medians per week:

| tier | season | fanBase | media | gate | **sponsorship** | total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| LOW | 2027 | 27,555 | $291K | $754K | $58K | $1.10M |
| LOW | 2046 | 142,485 (5.2×) | $568K | $2.60M | **$322K (5.6×)** | $3.49M |
| MID | 2027 | 64,690 | $638K | $1.59M | $139K | $2.36M |
| MID | 2046 | **664,180 (10.3×)** | $784K | $3.30M | **$1.47M (10.6×)** | $5.55M |
| POWER | 2027 | 131,563 | $999K | $2.81M | $345K | $4.15M |
| POWER | 2046 | 639,955 (4.9×) | $1.06M | $3.16M | **$1.43M (4.1×)** | $5.64M |

Sponsorship tracks `fanBase` at a ratio of 1.0 to 1.03 over twenty seasons —
`sponsorMarketValue = fanBase × $1.25 + …`, and `fanBase` is the one quantity in
the game with no ceiling. Media is bounded (prestige and press are 0–100), the
gate is bounded by capacity. Sponsorship is not.

This is the mirror of the failure the charter names. It is not currently a
defect — it is the designed reward for building a fan base — but it is the term
that makes the compounding in C4 permanent, and MID passing POWER on fan base by
season 20 (664,180 vs 639,955) is the visible symptom. Worth a decision rather
than a fix.

**Money with nowhere to go, restated.** Over the 20-season run at 24 programs the
median MID budget reaches $163.15M and POWER $310.26M with zero insolvency, an
untouched 45% margin ceiling, a NIL sink used at 2%, and facility spend that
tops out at 5 levels × 5 facilities. There is nothing left to buy. That is the
design problem the charter asks me to report even though nothing is broken.
