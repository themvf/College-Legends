## Verdict

**More playable in the loop, less trustworthy in the ledger.** The core of the
game held up under the first real measurement it has ever had: determinism passes
all three checks, order-independent markets hold byte-identically across five
command orderings and three program-iteration orders at 72 programs, the
scholarship cap holds at the boundary and one past it with zero orphans, every
one of 36 phase and offseason-step refusals names the rule that owns it, and
`prospectOdds` — the newest number in the game, never checked by anyone — is
calibrated at all ten deciles. Four of the five priority cards post exactly what
the week delivers, including the three nobody had ever checked. That is a lot of
new green.

Against that, the two things that got worse are both about numbers the player is
asked to trust. A cycle-2 fix silently reopened a cycle-1 defect, and the test
written to guard it could not see it because it ran at a scale where the new code
could not bite; that is the second time this cycle a guard test certified the
defect it was written against. And the economy has drifted: insolvency is at 7–9
of 72 by season five against a stated ~3, reproduced by me on fresh seeds, with
the count accelerating. **Nothing found this cycle blocks a career**, which is
the honest headline — but a player who reads a card now has one more reason not
to believe it, and that is the specific thing this project keeps having to fix.

**Recommendation: fix first, then ship to testers.** Three items gate it — the
priority-card divergence (already fixed), the insolvency drift, and the
recruiting input validation. None is large. The clarity work that cycle 2
identified remains the bigger obstacle to a useful beta than anything in this
report.

### A correction on the record

The first draft of this plan asserted that `941d42f` also changed
`refreshPreparation` to clear the scouting refund marker at the week boundary,
and that this moved every program's weekly filing and therefore every game's
readiness. **That was wrong, and it was my error to carry.**

`refreshPreparation` has always rebuilt preparation with
`autoScoutedOpponentId: null`; `git show 941d42f^:packages/simulation/src/index.ts`
shows it, and I have now confirmed it. The commit changed a comment there and
nothing else. The implementer had reported the marker as a fixed defect after an
A/B whose "before" arm contained a preservation they had just added themselves,
found the error while re-establishing the determinism baseline, and corrected it
in `8502330`.

My part in it is worth stating, because the plan is the thing on the record.
I read the `packages/` diff before planning — and misread a comment-only hunk as
a behaviour change, because the two lines that mattered
(`autoScoutedOpponentId: null`, `autoScoutedPoints: 0`) appeared in the diff as
*context*, not as additions. I then built Brief E's baseline work and Brief D's
headline justification on top of it. **A "Fix" section in an issue file is a
claim, not evidence**, and a diff read for its comments rather than its `+`
lines is not a diff read. Two of my own standing rules — measure, don't assert;
a diagnosis is a hypothesis — apply to the lead as much as to a tester.

One thing worth keeping from the episode, because it is a use for the baseline
nobody had claimed: **an unchanged determinism hash across nineteen commits is
what exposed an inert engine change.** That is a second job for target E, and it

### Brief A — gameplay-tester: recruiting end to end, in the browser

**Scale.** Two careers, both driven in the real app at 72 programs:

| career | path | why this one |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Program Riser** | the middle of the range; the default recruiting experience |
| 2 | **Dynasty Builder** | opens on a $1.5M budget, so the price curve actually bites and an unaffordable prospect is reachable |

At least **two full seasons plus an offseason** each, ideally into the third —
a signed class does not enrol until `completeOffseason`, so a career that stops
at signing day has not seen recruiting resolve. Record the web seed
(`web-alpha-<path>-<reroll>`) for both.

**What to do, specifically.**

1. **Exercise every recruiting action at least three times each** and record,
   for each one: what you spent, what changed on screen, and which event fired.
   `OFFER_PROSPECT`, `SCHEDULE_VISIT`, `SET_NIL_OFFER`, buying an evaluation,
   spending pursuit points. The question is not whether it is balanced — it is
   whether **anything observable happened**. `expected-behavior.md` §5: a
   behaviour that cannot be observed in an event is worth filing, because the
   player cannot see it either.
2. **Follow one prospect from first sighting to enrolment.** Record the posted
   odds each week, the money asked, the money offered, who else was bidding, and
   the outcome. Then find him on the roster the next season. If he is not there,
   that is a P1-class finding — say so.
3. **Money.** An accepted NIL offer is charged weekly. Cross-check the Finances
   panel against the `WEEKLY_FINANCES` event for three separate weeks after a
   signing. A number on the recruiting screen that the ledger does not charge is
   a §3 breach.
4. **The scholarship cap in the UI.** Recruit toward the cap and report what the
   screen says when you reach it, and what it says if you try to exceed it.
5. **Issue 01 regression, by hand.** The recruiting board with a mix of
   evaluated and unevaluated rows: click **every** row. This crashed the app in
   cycle 2 and the fix has been verified only by me, on a fixture and one career.
6. **The nine web-screen fixes.** Each has a stated reproduction in its issue
   file. Run each once and report *held* or *returned*:
   `2026-09-05-03` (scheme change price, fit not a score), `-04` (depth chart
   names the scheme's starters), `-05` (inbox), `-06` (coach candidate install
   number), `-07` (Development screen reachable, and the attribute is the
   player's choice — check all three routes), `-08` (postgame for the last game
   of the season **and** the playoff), `-09` (offseason navigation, all five
   steps), `-13` (error boundary — provoke a render error if you can find a
   legitimate way, otherwise just confirm it exists), `-14` (national press has
   one name everywhere).
7. **Reload mid-career and resume**, twice: once mid-season, once mid-offseason.
   Confirm you come back on the same screen with the same state.
8. **Latency per week**, every week. Compare against `game-balance.md` §7:
   ~2.1s early, ~3.5s by season four. **A regression above 2× is a P1**; the
   existence of the latency is not a finding.

**Pass looks like.** No crash, no page error, no console error. Every recruiting
action produces something observable. The nine fixes hold. Latency inside 2× of
§7. The followed prospect ends up on a roster.

**Check these before filing anything.**

- `expected-behavior.md`'s not-a-defect list, and `game-balance.md` §6.
- A **rejected command is working as designed** — `SET_WEEK_HOURS`,
  `SET_STAFF_ALLOCATION` and `SET_PRACTICE_REPS` are refused on purpose with

### Brief B — edge-case-tester: recruiting boundaries and the contested market

**Headless**, against `dist/`. Volume and constructed states, not the browser.
Seeds: `qa-c3-market-1`, `-2`, `-3` for the market work; name any others you use.

**1. Order independence — `expected-behavior.md` §2, never tested outside the
suite.** At **72 programs**, collect a week's worth of valid recruiting commands
(rival-planned plus a set of your own), then resolve the same set three ways:
forward, reversed, and shuffled with a fixed permutation. Assert the resulting
prospect state is **byte-identical** across all three. Repeat on three seeds.
Do the same for the portal step. A single winner that changes with command order
is a **P1** and I want it isolated to a prospect id if it happens.

**2. The scholarship cap.** Reach the limit legitimately, then:
attempt one more signing; attempt to enrol a class that would exceed it at
`completeOffseason`; check for an orphaned prospect (signed, never enrolled,
never withdrawn). State whether you reached the state through commands or
constructed it.

**3. Invalid and degenerate recruiting input.** Each must be **refused with a
reason that names the right owner**, not silently dropped:

- an offer to a prospect who is already committed elsewhere, and to one
  committed to you;
- an offer from a program at the cap;
- an unknown prospect id; a command for **another program**;
- a negative NIL offer, a zero offer, an offer above the program's entire
  budget, a fractional one;
- spending pursuit points you do not have; spending at exactly zero;
- a recruiting command in `ROSTER_REVIEW`, in `OFFSEASON`, and in the wrong
  offseason step.

**4. `prospectOdds` at its boundaries.** Zero contenders; not pursuing;
committed here; committed elsewhere; a program that cannot afford the ask;
`nilOffer` at 0 and at the budget. Assert the percentage is finite, inside
0–100, and never `NaN`. A percentage above 100 or a `NaN` is a finding at any
sample size.

**5. Timing boundaries.** `SIGNING_WEEK`; the week-14 rollover; the final
offseason step into a new season; and **week 1 of season 1**, where there is no
prior week to read — this is where a rule that depends on history silently does
nothing, and the scouting tiers are deliberately gated on `filmGames > 0`.
Confirm the gate fires and that the file still pays readiness from the first
point.

**6. One interaction, because recruiting and the portal share a market.** A
program bidding in the portal *and* recruiting in the same offseason, including
a retention bid by the program the player is leaving.

**Pass looks like.** No throw. No silent no-op. Every refusal names the phase,
step or rule that owns it. No impossible number. No state with no legal next
action.

**Check these before filing.** A correct, well-explained refusal is the system
working — do not file it. A state you wrote directly into `state` that the
engine could never reach is not a bug, but **say which you did** for every
finding; "I believed it was unreachable and it was not" is itself the more
interesting result.

### Brief C — economy-tester: the ledger over a dynasty

**Scale.** Two runs, both headless:

| run | scale | for |
|---|---|---|
| C-long | **20 seasons**, 24 programs, **two seeds** (`qa-c3-econ-a`, `qa-c3-econ-b`) | the dynasty questions; 24 keeps it affordable at 20 seasons |
| C-wide | **5 seasons, 72 programs**, one seed (`qa-c3-econ-72`) | the insolvency band is stated as "~3 **of 72** by season five" and can only be checked at that scale |

**1. The three failures your charter names, in order.** Each shipped once.

- **Winning must pay more than losing.** Split programs by record each season;
  compare budget change. **The worst winning season must beat the best losing
  one.** A failure here is a **P1 at any sample size** — no pooling argument
  applies.
- **Nothing may scale with an unbounded quantity.** Hold everything constant,
  multiply `fanBase` thirtyfold, confirm the cost base does not move.
- **Reserves must not become permanent cost.** Raise opening balances in a
  scratch run; insolvency must *improve*. If it worsens, something is converting
  cash into recurring cost, which is exactly how the last collapse worked.

**2. The series.** Per tier, per season: median budget, weekly net, insolvency
count, and **expense composition** (squad / facilities / stadium / operations).
A term at 60%+ of expenses is the smell that localised the runaway-in-reverse —
report it even if nothing else looks wrong.

**3. The question this cycle actually exists to answer.** Recruiting is the
intended money sink. **Measure how much money actually leaves through it** —
NIL commitments and recruiting spend as a share of revenue, per tier, per
season, over the 20-season run. Then state plainly whether the POWER
compounding curve (2.5× over four seasons in §6; 6.7× over twelve in the soak)
is changed by it. This is the number cycle 2 said could not be interpreted until
recruiting was live.

**4. Posted versus charged.** For three separate weeks in each run, compare the
figures `operatingCost`, `mediaRights` and facility upkeep post against what
`WEEKLY_FINANCES` actually charges. They must agree **exactly**. The finance
panel once recomputed operating cost from an approximation and understated it;
that class of defect has produced half the P1/P2s in this project. Also check
the inverse: **a budget movement that no `WEEKLY_FINANCES` event accounts for.**

**Pass looks like.** Winning beats losing in every season measured. ~3 of 72
insolvent by season 5. No unbounded scaling. Posted equals charged to the
dollar. No expense term dominating.

**Check these before filing.** A LOW program drifting slowly negative is
intended. POWER growing ~2.5× over four seasons is known — report a *change* in
it, with the season count stated, not its existence. A rival pricing inside the
0.90×–1.18× cohort band but not optimally is by design. Money is yours; football
rates are not.

### Brief D — balance-tester: competitiveness, slot value, and odds calibration

Three measurements. **Priority order is D3, then D2, then D1** — the two that
test systems nobody has ever measured come before the one that re-measures a
system nothing has changed. D1 is the droppable one.

**D1 — A pooled competitiveness baseline over six leagues. Lowest priority of
the three; drop this one first if the cycle runs short.**
72 programs, one season each, seeds `qa-c3-pool-1` … `qa-c3-pool-6`. Report,
pooled and per-league: one-score-game rate, home win rate, average margin,
shutout rate, points per team-game. Six leagues because per-league variance on
the one-score rate is ±3.5 points on 144 games and the stated floor has only 1.4
points of headroom.

**This is establishing a baseline, not hunting a regression, and the
justification changed after the plan's first draft.** It was originally here
because I believed `941d42f` had moved league-wide readiness. It did not — that
commit cannot reach a game. Nothing since cycle 2 has touched game resolution,
so **no move is expected**. It survives on an independent reason that predates
my error: `game-balance.md` §2 requires six leagues for the one-score rate,
`CLAUDE.md` says in its own words that the existing four-league pool "is
under-powered and should pool six", and **no `balance-tester` has ever run**, so
every figure in §2 is currently quoted rather than independently measured.
Doing it now also gives cycle 4's simulation-accuracy work a neutral reference
to open against.

Say explicitly whether the pooled one-score rate is inside or outside its floor,
and give the per-league spread so the next cycle knows how much of it is noise.
**The finding is a figure outside its band, not the existence of a deviation
already listed in §6.**

**D2 — Issue 12: what a priority slot is actually worth.**
The open issue's evidence is one program at one capacity, and part of it is
**stale in a specific way you need to know about before you measure.**

`941d42f` changed what the scouting card posts — one of the two cards the issue
says can never win a slot. Per the verified-fixed table in issue
`2026-09-05-02`, that card moved from `leave +1.9 / priority +2.1`, where both
branches delivered `1.46`, to `leave +1.2 / priority +1.5`, where the branches
genuinely differ. So:

- The issue's absolute figures for that row are **stale**. Re-measure them; do
  not re-cite them.
- The issue's *marginal* value for that row was `+0.3` and may still be `+0.3`,
  in which case its core claim survives its own stale table. **Do not assume
  either way** — that is what the grid is for.
- The issue's second supporting bullet — *"the delivered readiness is identical
  whether or not the slot is spent"* — is **fixed and no longer true**. That was
  the strongest claim in the issue, so the finding you are measuring is weaker
  than the one that was filed.

Grid: **3 tiers × 3 focus capacities (1, 2, 3 slots) × 5 cards × 3 weeks**
(week 1, week 7, week 13 — film accumulates and stakes move), pooled over **four
leagues**, at least three programs per tier per league. Method per cell:
`prepareWeek` with `focuses=[]` against `focuses=[card]`, read `weekPriorities()`
both sides.

Two outputs, and the second matters more than the first:

- **(a) Marginal value of one slot, per card, per cell.** Is any card dominated
  at *every* cell, or does the picture change by tier, capacity or week? A card
  that is dominated everywhere is a dead decision; a card that wins somewhere is
  a design working and issue 12 gets argued down.
- **(b) Posted versus delivered, for all five cards.** Advance the week and read
  the `WEEK_FOCUS_PAYOFF` event. Only `SCOUT` and `RECRUIT` have ever been
  checked, and **both were wrong** when they were. The other three
  — `INSTALL_OFFENSE`, `INSTALL_DEFENSE`, `DEVELOP` — have never been checked by
  anyone. Any gap here is a §3 breach and is **P1 by default**.

Also count, over four leagues × one season: **how often the dashboard briefing
flags a card worth ≥65 that is not being chased.** The design leans on that
re-prompt to make standing priorities safe. Cycle 2's claim that it "never fired
usefully" is an eighteen-week anecdote; give it an *n*.

**D3 — `prospectOdds` calibration. The headline measurement of this cycle.**
The recruiting row posts a percentage. Nobody has ever checked it against an
outcome.

Method: at the moment odds are posted for a (program, prospect) pair, record the
percentage; then record whether that program signed him. Bucket into deciles.
Sample: **four leagues × one season of recruiting at 72 programs**, target
**≥2,000 posted-odds observations** — state what you actually got.

Pass: observed signing frequency inside each decile lands within the decile
±10 points, and the relationship is **monotone**. A monotonicity break is a real
bug rather than tuning — "more of an input producing less of an output" is on
your own finding list, and it would mean the number the whole screen is built
around is not a probability.

**Check these before filing.** `game-balance.md` §6 — do not re-file a known
deviation. A single-league result is not a finding. Money is `economy-tester`'s.
Per-game football rates are **cycle 4's** — if D1 shows one outside §1's ±8%,
report it as an observation and flag it for me, do not chase it.

### Brief E — regression-tester: determinism, the baseline, and the closed corpus

**Runs first, before the other four are dispatched.**

**E1 — The three determinism checks.** Same-process replay; save round trip;
cross-change replay. Any failure is **P1**.

**E2 — Confirm the established baseline. Do not re-derive it.**
The baseline is **not** stale — an earlier draft of this plan said it was, on a
false premise of mine, and the implementer has already re-measured it at
`d022c4b` as `55e8ac644f4875c2`, unchanged from cycle 2. This is one run and one
comparison, using the exact recorded method so it is like-for-like: seed
`qa-baseline-2026-09`, **72 programs, 3 seasons**, sha256 of
`JSON.stringify({programs, players, seasonHistory})`, first 16 hex.

- **Unchanged** is the expected result and a PASS. Record it and move on.
- **Changed** is a finding, and a serious one, because nothing in the ten
  commits should be able to move it. Localise where state first diverges before
  filing.

**E3 — Ask whether the baseline scenario actually exercises what changed. This
replaces a diff-attribution task that no longer has a diff to attribute.**

Exactly one commit can reach a result — `d022c4b`'s incumbent filter, which
changes what `staffCandidatesFor` shows the rival planner — and the baseline
hash across it is *unchanged*. Both readings are plausible and they have
opposite consequences:

- **Benign:** the filter removes a candidate the planner would never have hired
  anyway (the incumbent offers no rating improvement over himself), so no hire
  changes and the hash is rightly identical.
- **Vacuous:** the three-season scenario does not exercise the coaching market
  enough to show it, in which case the unchanged hash says nothing about that
  commit at all.

Distinguish them: count how many coaching changes the baseline scenario actually
makes, and confirm at least one market was opened where the incumbent would have
appeared pre-filter. This is exactly the check that just caught an inert engine
change — the implementer asked "does this scenario exercise scouting?", found it
did, and that is how the false defect surfaced. Ask the same question of the
staff market. **An unchanged hash is only evidence if the path was walked.**

**E4 — Measure the one real behavioural change: coaching churn.**
Changes per program per year, either side of `d022c4b`, over **≥4 leagues × ≥3
seasons**. `CLAUDE.md` records 0.52 after the last change to this candidate list
and notes it tripled from 0.18 the time before, so this list has a history of
moving league churn more than expected. A material move is a finding even though
the fix itself is correct.

*(The scouting-readiness measurement that used to sit here is deleted. It was
premised on `941d42f` changing what programs file, which it does not — the
commit's surviving half is a projection function the engine never reads.)*

| `2026-09-05-02` scouting card | eight weeks, posted within 0.05 of delivered, **and the two branches differ in at least three of them**. Verify only the card half — the marker half of that issue was withdrawn in `8502330` as a defect that never existed, and the issue file records the correction |

## Process — four of five briefs were killed by rate limits

Recorded because it changed what this cycle produced, and because it will happen
again.

**Briefs A, B, C and D were each killed at least once by an account rate limit
and resumed from scratch files on disk.** B was killed twice. D's collection ran
on 2026-09-05 and its analysis on 2026-09-06. Triage itself was killed mid-pass
and resumed.

What survived, and why: **every brief that had been writing intermediate results
to `qa/scratch/` as it went recovered completely.** B transcribed whole sections
from `t1.log`, `t2-cap.log` and `t3.log`, which were produced before the first
kill; D re-ran only its analysis pass over committed row files; C re-derived from
surviving run data.

What it cost, in order of size:

1. **Brief A never reached the recruiting loop** — the one system this cycle was
   planned around. The browser brief is the most expensive and least resumable
   of the five: a Playwright session and a career in progress are both lost on a
   kill, and there is no partial artefact to resume from the way there is for a
   headless row file.
2. **Brief C produced a summary without its evidence**, so six of its eight lines
   cannot be adjudicated and two had to be re-measured from scratch by me.
3. Two of the three P2s in this report were reproduced by the lead rather than
   accepted from a run log, which is the correct outcome but was forced rather
   than chosen.

Two things to change before cycle 4, both cheap:

- **Browser briefs should check in after every career milestone**, not at the
  end. A run log that stops at "preseason 2027" after a kill is a run log with
  four confirmed results in it; the same brief writing per-screen would have kept
  twenty.
- **A run log's summary table must not be written before its evidence.** C's
  summary reads as eight findings and is currently two. That is not the
  reporter's error — it is a template that invites a headline ahead of its
  support — and the `qa/runs/_template.md` ordering should be inverted.
