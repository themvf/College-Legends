College Legends · three direction trials · one board, three treatments

Same screen, three grounds

The identical content in all three: four prospects on a recruiting board with an overall range, a price and odds, plus one of them opened up. Anything that differs between these is a design decision, not a content difference — which is the only way to actually judge them.

01

Bloomberg × Moneyball

"scout terminal"

The least contradictory of the three: both halves are professional tooling that refuses to be pretty. Bloomberg supplies the density and the idea that each colour is a channel with a meaning; Moneyball pulls every hue down to institutional. Density is the argument — the screen is full because the job is serious.

Its best argument

Density reads as respect. It treats the player as a professional with a job, and the game genuinely does have this much information. Nothing is hidden behind a friendly card.

What kills it

It is hostile to a new player and worse on a phone. Eleven-pixel monospace in six panels is not an iOS product. This is the desktop-only direction.

Where it lands

The most credible of the three and the least likely to be loved. Good bones for a "pro view" toggle; wrong as the default.

02

Bloomberg × Arcade

"the floor"

Real Bloomberg is already half arcade — hot orange on pure black, four hues shouting at once. This one stops apologising for that. Full chroma, bloom on anything live, a function-key rail that reads like a cabinet's control panel. Same density as above; none of the restraint.

Its best argument

The most distinctive of the three by a wide margin, and the only one anybody would screenshot. It makes a recruiting table feel like something is at stake right now.

What kills it

Fatigue. Four saturated hues plus bloom for the twelfth week running is exhausting, and every colour is already spoken for — there is no headroom left to signal anything new.

Where it lands

Strongest as a moment, weakest as a home. Consider it for the postgame and the draft-night screens rather than for the board you sit on all season.

03

Palantir × Arcade

"case file"

The sharpest tension and, I think, the best fit for what this game actually is. Palantir's grammar is investigative — entities, links, provenance, a lot of air, one cool accent. Arcade supplies exactly one thing: when the investigation lands, the finding lights up. Nothing else on the screen is ever allowed to.

Two of three served. A visit is worth +4.1 here against +2.6 on Ngata, because a visit scales with fit.

Its best argument

It matches a system the game already has. Hype-versus-truth, evaluation tiers, dossiers, the diamond in the rough — those are investigative findings. This is the only direction whose visual grammar is the game's own logic.

What kills it

It needs findings to exist. On a week where you discover nothing, the accent never fires and the screen is austere to the point of cold. It also flatters the scouting systems over the football.

Where it lands

My recommendation. It scales to mobile, costs no illustration, and the one hot colour keeps its meaning because it is rationed — which is exactly what the other two spend on their first screen.

—

If you only take one thing

All three are cheap: type, palette, layout, no illustration. What separates them is how much of the colour budget they spend before anything has happened. Bloomberg × Moneyball spends it on structure, Bloomberg × Arcade spends it all immediately, and Palantir × Arcade keeps almost all of it in reserve so that one number lighting up still means something in season twelve. That is the whole difference, and it is why the third one is the one I would build.
