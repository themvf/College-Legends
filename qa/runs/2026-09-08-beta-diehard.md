## What was fun

1. **Film study created a specific football plan.** Caleb's weak accuracy alongside good pocket movement and reading defenses made him more than a single overall rating. I chose him over a last-year QB2 and trained the exact weakness. Week 4's improvement made that choice feel productive.
2. **A performance could become an agency decision.** A one-point win and player-of-week recognition produced Spotlight. Choosing LIGHT gave Caleb enough NIL income to complete the ambition with less fatigue; the reward then included trust, prestige, and a referral. That is the kind of interconnection I would return for.
3. **Micah's rival contest and career payoff had stakes.** Fieldhouse competed on the same service promise. I lowered commission and paid for dedicated attention, won him, reached his development ambition, and saw him drafted. Failing the later roster cut prevented the result from being a completely automatic success story.

## What was not fun

1. **Client requests did not seem to remember recent care.** Caleb repeated the starting-player investment request at Week 6 after two training blocks and a paid position-coaching response. Micah used essentially the same framing. I adopted a mechanical free-response rule instead of reading each one as a personal story.
2. **An unsuccessful training block offered little to think about.** “No measurable gain this block; reassess the plan” did not explain what caused the miss or which changed choice might help. My answer was simply to buy the same training again. Uncertainty is useful; an unexplained null result is less engaging.
3. **The football season was too thin in the route I played.** Caleb's 4–8 year largely arrived as separate scores/stat lines. Micah often registered four tackles and zero sacks. I wanted more context around opponent importance, team trajectory, role security, and what scouts thought of performances. These may exist elsewhere; the main loop did not bring them to me.

## Confusing or missing

1. **Career intent starts in an unexpected place.** Caleb defaulted to “Enter draft” despite three seasons remaining and a developmental signing story. Return became actionable after Week 12. I needed a clear reminder and a rationale for the default, or an undecided/develop-here starting intention.
2. **Two coaching routes communicate progress differently.** Community training offers uncertain skill gain converted three-to-one into overall; request coaching offers a guaranteed +1 ability. In this session the first paid training yielded zero while the request purchase worked immediately. Their different purpose and cause are not sufficiently clear to me.
3. **Important spending decisions need the right comparison at the decision point.** The first campaign's $16,036 gross became $13,631 take-home against a $15,000 net ambition. Pro Preparation displayed a detailed payout ladder and large package prices while my client's career assessment remained unresearched. I could see costs and eventual rewards more easily than my client's relevant net benefit and current outlook.

## Preserve

- Position-specific skills, uncertain potential ranges, and playing-time context in film reports.
- The player earnings / agency commission distinction and manageable early campaign costs.
- Performance-triggered sponsor choices with lighter/full workload alternatives.
- One final rival counter where commission and personal support express the agency's priorities.
- Ambitions that pay off with trust, prestige, and referrals; agency unlocks that follow actual client progress.
- A drafted player's separate roster outcome and the ability to continue with a returning college client.

## Continue / quit moments and return verdict

**First wanted to continue:** Caleb's preseason film report: accuracy 60, reading defenses 72, pocket movement 75, potential 71–91, three seasons, and starting snaps. I wanted to find out whether I could make this athletic starter a pro quarterback.

**Strongest reinforcement:** Week 4's 25–24 first win, player-of-week recognition, Accuracy 61→66.4, and an earned sponsor offer. Those events joined the football and agency stories.

**First wanted to quit:** Week 6, when Caleb repeated his starting-player investment request despite recent paid coaching and two training blocks. I continued the test, but switched to a standing free-response policy because the interaction no longer felt personal.

**Would I play again?** Yes, for Caleb's second year and to try a different recruiting risk. This is qualified interest, not a claim of long-term retention. The roster-cut ending leaves room for meaningful uncertainty; repeated requests and thin weekly football context are the main threats to my interest across multiple seasons.
