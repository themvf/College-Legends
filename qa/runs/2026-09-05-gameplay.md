### FINDING A — a committed recruit's own report says "No NIL offer is active", and the screen lets you book the same money twice

**Career 1, `web-alpha-program_riser-0`, Everglades State Pythons. Season 2027,
week 3.** Screenshot `09-theo-committed.png`.

Repro from a fresh start:

1. Program Riser → Everglades State Pythons → keep the four incumbents →
   `This is my football team` → `Accept roster & begin season`.
2. Recruiting → select **Theo Wright** (RB, NATIONAL, FL) → `Offer scholarship`
   → `Basic · 5 RP`. Advance week 1.
3. Recruiting → Theo Wright → NIL slider to `$2.2K` → `Queue $2K / week` →
   `Invest 20 RP` → `Character · 8 RP`. Advance week 2.
4. Recruiting → Theo Wright. He is now **Committed to you**.

At step 4 the following three things are on the screen **at the same time**:

```
HUD          PLAYER PAY COMMITTED   $2K      $2K a week reserved
Scout facts  "No NIL offer is active."
Weekly NIL   Offer amount $0 / week   "No NIL offer is active."
             Wants $2K per week
```

The engine is right and the screen is wrong. On commitment
`packages/simulation/src/index.ts` moves the winning offer out of
`offersByProspect` and into `commitmentsByPlayer` — the code comment says so
("once he is committed here the same dollars live in `commitmentsByPlayer`
instead") and `recruitingScore` reads the fallback explicitly:

```ts
const nilOffer = nilOverride
  ?? state.nil?.[programId]?.offersByProspect[prospect.id]
  ?? state.nil?.[programId]?.commitmentsByPlayer[prospect.id]
  ?? 0;
```

`apps/web/src/recruiting-view-model.ts` has no such fallback —
`currentNilOffer` and `effectiveNilOffer` read `offersByProspect` only:

```ts
const currentNilOffer = state.nil?.[programId]?.offersByProspect[prospect.id] ?? 0;
```

**The second half is the one that costs the player money.** Because the slider
resets to `$0` on a recruit the program is already paying $2K/week for, the
obvious next action is to put the offer back. I did, with real keyboard input on
an enabled control (`ArrowRight ×40`, driver reports `before "0" / after
"2000"`), and clicked `Queue $2K / week`. The HUD then read:

```
before   PLAYER PAY (NIL) AVAILABLE  $48K   $46K a week still free
         PLAYER PAY COMMITTED        $2K    $0 a week reserved
after    PLAYER PAY (NIL) AVAILABLE  $48K   $44K a week still free
         PLAYER PAY COMMITTED        $2K    $2K a week reserved
```

**One recruit, counted twice against donor capacity, $4K of a $49K ceiling.**
It survives the week — at week 4 it still reads `committed $2K · $2K reserved ·
$44K free`. Nothing on screen warns; the panel actively told me there was no
offer to duplicate.

The weekly ledger charges $2K, not $4K, so this is lost *capacity* rather than
lost cash — but donor capacity is the recruiting budget, so a player working a
board will silently lose room for other recruits. I read this as one defect with
two symptoms; the lead may want to split it.

`prospect.status === "COMMITTED"` also leaves the whole decision stack live —
Rescind, more evaluations, more pursuit points, another visit. Defending a
commitment before `SIGNING_WEEK` is legitimate, so I am not filing that part;
it is only the NIL control that misstates the state.

### FINDING B — the inbox reports 71 other programs' NIL offers as the player's own

**Career 1, same seed. Season 2027, week 4, Dashboard → "What happened".**
Screenshot `11-nil-offer-resolved-not-mine.png`.

Three rows, verbatim:

```
✓  Nil Offer Resolved   Nicholas Reed took your $850 a week offer.
✓  Nil Offer Resolved   Justin Adams took your $850 a week offer.
✓  Nil Offer Resolved   Nico Matthews took your $850 a week offer.
```

I never made an $850 offer to anybody. My only NIL offer all career is $2K to
Theo Wright. Checked in the same session: none of those three names is on my
30-row board, and my ledger reads `committed $2K · reserved $2K`.

Cause: `eventRelevantToProgram` in `App.tsx` scopes a named list of event types
by `event.programId === programId`, and `NIL_OFFER_RESOLVED` is not on it, so it
falls through to `return true`. The event *carries* `programId`
(`packages/simulation/src/index.ts` sets it on every emit) — the UI simply does
not read it. The copy then hard-codes the second person:

```ts
if (event.result === "WON") return `${prospect} took your ${offer} offer.`;
```

**This is issue `2026-09-05-05` returning in a different event type.** That
issue was "the inbox is other programs' booster offers under an unrelated
sentence", and its fix added `BOOSTER_OFFERED`/`BOOSTER_RESOLVED` to this exact
list with a comment explaining why. `NIL_OFFER_RESOLVED` was missed, and it is
worse than the booster case was, because the sentence says *your*.

I checked the neighbouring types while I was here: `RECRUITING_CONTEST_RESOLVED`
**is** scoped (on `offeredBy`), `PROSPECT_COMMITTED` / `NIL_DEAL_SIGNED` /
`PROSPECT_SIGNED` / `PROSPECT_FLIPPED` are scoped to the program or to prospects
the player has discovered. `NIL_OFFER_RESOLVED` and `NIL_COMMITMENT_ENDED` are
the pair to look at — the latter *is* scoped, the former is not.

### Item 3 — NIL charged weekly, Finances against `WEEKLY_FINANCES`

The panel is not a re-derivation: `App.tsx` reads the last `WEEKLY_FINANCES`
event for the program and prints its fields, so the two cannot drift by
construction. What I could check is that the recruiting screen's committed
figure is the figure the ledger charges, and that the charge repeats.

Theo Wright committed during the week-2 advance. The engine's own comment says
the drain begins at commitment rather than enrollment, and it does:

| season / week charged | "NIL commitments" line | recruiting HUD "Player pay committed" |
|---|---|---|
| 2027 wk 2 | **$2K** | $2K |
| 2027 wk 3 | **$2K** | $2K |
| 2027 wk 4 | (below) | $2K |

Week 2's ledger also reconciles internally to the dollar:
`128K + 467K + 150K + 276K + 48K + 2K = $1,071K` against the posted `$1.1M of
costs`, and `$501K revenue − $1,071K = −$570K`, the posted net.

Note the duplicate reservation from Finding A is **not** double-charged — the
ledger charges $2K while the HUD reserves a second $2K. That is the right
behaviour for the cash and the wrong behaviour for the capacity, and it is what
makes Finding A a capacity bug rather than a billing one.

**One limit on this check, stated so it is not over-read:** every money figure
in the web UI is rendered through `money()`, which rounds to 0.1M above a
million, so "In the bank $6.0M" cannot resolve a $570K weekly net. I can
reconcile the expense breakdown against its own total exactly, and I can confirm
the NIL line repeats weekly, but I cannot audit the budget movement to the
dollar from the browser. That check belongs to `economy-tester` headless.
