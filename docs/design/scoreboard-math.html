The numbers are the art

Moneyball and arcade pull opposite ways on restraint, and agree completely on one thing: a number is the most exciting object on the screen. One puts it on a printout, the other on a high-score board. That overlap is the whole direction — and it happens to suit a game whose actual content is six thousand players and a ledger.

The hue does the work

Amber CRT was what 1980s arcade cabinets used and what 1980s data terminals used. It is the one colour that is honestly both halves of this brief, which is why it carries every figure in the product and nothing else does.

Ground · Moneyball

Desaturated slate with a green bias — a locker room under fluorescent light. Sober, unglamorous, never competing with data.

#0B1113 #121A1C #26343E

Signal · Arcade

Amber on every number, cyan for a second series, and a hard green/red only for direction. Nothing decorative is ever allowed these.

#FFB000 #3ED8E8 #3DD68C #FF6B6B

Type · both at once

Monospace is the joke that makes this cohere: it is a spreadsheet and a terminal. Every figure sits in it, tabular. Headlines take a heavy condensed marquee face; prose stays a quiet sans and small.

Surface · arcade weight

Two- and three-pixel rules instead of hairlines, sunken wells for data, a scanline at 22% on the scoreboard only, amber bloom only on a hero figure. A whisper of CRT, never a filter.

Where it fights, and what it costs

Three real risks, stated up front rather than discovered at implementation.

Risk 1 · arcade is maximalist and this game is dense

A box score is roughly forty figures per team. Give all of them glow and the screen is unreadable neon. The discipline has to be absolute: exactly one hero figure per screen gets bloom, everything else is flat amber. If that rule slips even slightly, the direction collapses into a slot machine.

Risk 2 · CRT texture ages badly and hurts small screens

Scanlines at full strength are a 2014 indie tic and they wreck legibility at iOS text sizes. Used here at 22% on one component only. The honest version of this direction may ship with no scanline at all — the amber and the segmented bars carry it without any texture.

Risk 3 · it is a skin, and skins can hide a thin game

This looks like a lot of design for very little art budget, which is its main virtue — it is typography, palette and layout, no illustration. But a high-score treatment on a number nobody chose is still a number nobody chose. It amplifies the decision systems; it cannot substitute for them.
