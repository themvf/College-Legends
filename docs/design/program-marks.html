College Legends · identity study · five of seventy-two

One shield, seventy-two programs

Five marks drawn from real programs in the league — one per character archetype, one per division. The point is not five drawings. It is a system cheap enough to carry all seventy-two and specific enough that each one still reads as a place.

Shape says division

Six silhouettes, one per division. A heraldic point for the Atlantic, an industrial plate for the Great Lakes, a broad badge for the Heartland, a rounded shield for the Gulf, a chevron for the Mountain.

Glyph says place

One idea, drawn flat: a keystone, a storm, a skyline, a crescent, a summit. Never a mascot illustration — a mascot is a drawing per program, a glyph is a shape you can generate.

Colour says character

A blueblood gets old gold and a drawn border. A frontrunner gets near-black and one hot accent. A developer gets restraint. Same system, five different postures.

Band says who

Every mark closes on the same lettered band, which is what makes a wall of seventy-two feel like one league rather than seventy-two hobby projects.

Dark shields get a keyline

Found by drawing these on both grounds rather than one: a steel or near-black shield disappears into a dark UI. Any shield darker than the ground carries a 3-unit rule in its own accent. Lake Erie and Lone Star have one; the other three do not need it.

The five

Atlantic · Power · Blueblood

Keystone Commonwealth Founders

Harrisburg, Pennsylvania

The state's own nickname happens to be an architectural shape, so the glyph is the arch itself with the keystone seated in its apex — a wedge drawn on its own reads as a cup, and it took two attempts to find that out. The mortar joints cut down its sloping sides are what settle it as stone. The gold inner rule is the blueblood tell: the only mark of the five that frames itself, because old money does.

#10233F #C9A227
Keystone Commonwealth W 31–17

Great Lakes · Power · Diehard

Lake Erie Storm

Cleveland, Ohio

Three bars for the lake, fading back, and the bolt driven straight down through all of them — the nickname and the geography in one figure. The flat-topped plate and the steel grey are the diehard posture: this is a base that turns up in November rain, so the mark should look like it was stamped rather than designed.

#2B353D #4FA3D1 #F4F7F9
Lake Erie L 20–24

Heartland · Power · Frontrunner

Lone Star Metropolitan Wranglers

Dallas, Texas

Both halves of the name, stacked: a rising skyline for Metropolitan, the star sitting on top of it for Lone Star. The frontrunner treatment is the highest contrast of the five — near-black, white, and one hot red, nothing in between. A bandwagon program should look like a brand, because that is what its fans are buying.

#121417 #E03131 #F5F6F7
Lone Star Metropolitan W 45–38

Gulf · Power · Talent magnet

Crescent City Krewe

New Orleans, Louisiana

The one mark where the city hands you everything: a literal crescent, and the parade palette. The crescent is cut by a second disc in the shield colour rather than by a mask, which is deliberately dull engineering — it renders identically everywhere and survives being drawn at eighteen pixels. Richest colour of the five, for the program that recruits on glamour.

#5B2A86 #D4A017 #1F7A4C
Crescent City W 27–13

Mountain · Power · Developer

Front Range Summit

Denver, Colorado

Three peaks with the summit in the middle and the snowline cut in as negative space. The chevron shield points down to a single apex, so the silhouette itself is a mountain before you read anything inside it. The developer posture is the quietest of the five — two muted colours, no accent — because a program that wins by teaching should not shout.

#1F4D3D #E8E2D5
Front Range L 17–21

What this costs, and what it does not

Six shapes, not seventy-two

The silhouette is authored once per division and reused. That is the whole reason the system scales to a full league without seventy-two illustration commissions.

Glyphs are the real work

Roughly twenty flat glyphs — mountain, star, wave, bird, anchor, crown, bolt — cover seventy-two programs with repeats spread across divisions so no two neighbours share one.

Palettes come from data

Two colours plus a neutral, picked per program and stored beside character and stateCode. No new art pipeline: it is two hex values in the content package.

It is honest at 18 pixels

The lettered band goes illegible small, and that is fine — silhouette and colour carry the schedule row. Anything that needs a mascot's face to be recognisable has already failed.
