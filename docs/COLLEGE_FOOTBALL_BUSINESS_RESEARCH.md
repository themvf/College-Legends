# College football business: reality before implementation

Research synthesis — September 7, 2026. Two research agents independently investigated athletic-department finances and scheduling/conference markets; the lead reviewed athlete compensation, licensing and commercial staffing. Sources are university, conference, NCAA and industry participant publications. Findings below distinguish documented examples from proposed game abstractions. This is not a claim of interviews with administrators or fans.

## Core recommendation

Keep college football. Put the player in charge of the football enterprise within a university athletic department, with delegated coaches and commercial staff. Success means competitive strength, sustainable funding, audience, reserves and university confidence. The institution owns the program; the user does not personally own players, all their commercial rights, or a freely transferable franchise.

Start with an existing modest program, inherited team, employees, conference and contracts. If retaining $600,000, treat it as a provisional discretionary allocation, not a researched full annual football budget. Select and benchmark the starting competition tier before assigning salaries, guarantees or facility prices. FCS-to-FBS movement must not be a simple promotion mechanic.

## Players belong inside the enterprise, with separate money flows

Institutional athlete payments and covered benefits under the House settlement differ from third-party NIL. The initial 2025-26 benefits cap is historical and should not be frozen as a current football-only allowance. Model the applicable rules by season. [NCAA implementation](https://www.ncaa.org/media-center-a-letter-from-ncaa-president-charlie-baker/)

NIL involves defined commercial activity such as endorsements, appearances or licensing. NCAA guidance distinguishes that from unsupported payments labeled NIL. Do not call a generic recruiting purchase an NIL deal. [NCAA NIL guidance](https://www.ncaa.org/student-athletes/name-image-likeness/)

Proposed separate ledgers: school operating funds; restricted capital gifts; institutional athlete commitments; external athlete NIL income. The UI can summarize these simply, but the simulation must not count a business paying an athlete as school revenue. Athletes retain agency and finite eligibility; transfers and contract terms can interrupt expected returns. Eligibility rules are changing, with transition cohorts: date the game's fictional rules rather than casually hardcoding old assumptions. [NCAA eligibility explanation](https://www.ncaa.org/eligibility-center/division-i-and-division-ii-age-based-eligibility-rules/)

## A connected game example

The following chain is a design proposal, not a claim that each outcome is automatic:

1. A season-ticket renewal campaign succeeds; early receipts improve liquidity, but also commit the school to future game delivery.
2. Larger attendance makes local sponsorship inventory more attractive, provided commercial staff can sell and service it.
3. Recurring surplus supports a coordinator contract or recruiting-support investment. The choice reduces flexibility elsewhere and may not improve results.
4. Better performance can strengthen renewals and donor confidence. A popular athlete creates licensed products and endorsement opportunities with separate rights and beneficiaries.
5. A donor offers restricted funding for premium seating. Matching cash, approvals, construction and future upkeep make acceptance a consequential commitment.
6. A bad season then tests whether renewal losses and lower variable income can coexist with the larger fixed cost base.
7. Sustained audience, investment and competitive credibility may attract a conference invitation. Added distributions must be weighed against travel, required investment and transition obligations.

Staff roles attach to these bottlenecks: ticket sales handles accounts/renewals; development handles donors/pledges; commercial staff handles sponsor inventory and delivery; legal/compliance handles rights, commitments and athlete rules; coaches/scouts handle talent evaluation and development. Institutions retain baseline services. No opening requirement to hire every department.

## Opening and progression

Recommended first choice: an inherited season-ticket renewal problem, with one modest campaign decision and explicit spending/expected-demand uncertainty. Keep the schedule and roster running. An open future scheduling slot becomes a subsequent road-guarantee versus home-date decision.

Systems should exist from day zero as inherited arrangements. Milestones introduce opportunities to renegotiate or expand them. Television does not suddenly begin to exist after ten games; a contract renewal or league offer gives the player new control. Donor staffing appears when there are more relationships than the inherited staff can serve, rather than because a timer reached week seven.

The interface should expose one consequential decision, cash available for new commitments, and the forecast effect. Full bookkeeping stays one level deeper. Simplicity is about what the player must do now, not removing the actual economics.

## Prototype corrections before further implementation

- Replace the quarterback purchase as the universal opening and remove single-player dominance from demand/results.
- Replace guaranteed annual alumni money with a documented base funding arrangement plus uncertain fundraising and restrictions.
- Replace the universal direct-manufacturing jersey model with explicit operating and rights choices.
- Replace arbitrary weekly unlocks with inherited contracts, capacity milestones and renewal dates.
- Replace the three-negative-weeks closure rule with disclosed university support limits, commitments, recovery deadlines and terminal failure conditions.
- Replace skipped offseason agency: recruiting, renewals, fundraising, staffing and future scheduling are major business decisions.
- Add contract duration, payment timing, termination consequences and capital funding before adding more disconnected upgrades.

## Remaining validation

Research supports the institutional model, not proof that it is fun or accepted by fans. Before another major rewrite, create three linked, numerically reconciled scenarios: a renewal campaign; a future guarantee/home-date choice; and a funded expansion followed by a bad season. Benchmark a small-program budget and real contracts. Then have actual diehard fans and, if accessible, an athletics business practitioner challenge the scenarios. Agent research is not a substitute for those people.

No game code was changed by this research pass. Earlier implementation documents describe the prototype as built; this memo identifies revisions rather than implying those revisions have shipped.
