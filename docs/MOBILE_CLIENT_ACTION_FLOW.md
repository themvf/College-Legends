# Mobile client action flow

September 8, 2026. Responds to the first-play iPhone feedback: excessive text, unclear career direction, hidden activities, silent commitments, and global promises.

## Player-facing loop

Sign a client → deliver their promise / earn a payday → renew representation → choose college offers or the draft → receive the result.

Home and each client hub show the next actionable step. Money and prestige remain visible, including inside decision sheets. Primary activities use a fixed phone navigation bar; More opens a labeled activity grid. Records, explanations and school details remain available below the decisions.

## Decisions and results

- Recruiting opens a pitch for the named prospect with an individual promise and commission. Opening or cancelling spends nothing. Sending produces a signing, rejection or rival-counter result; signing leads directly to that client's hub.
- Sponsor offers use Review, Confirm commitment, and a receipt with the cost now, client earnings, agency earnings and delivery time. These are fixed offers, not a negotiation mechanic. Active campaigns lock competing campaigns until completion. Payment generates a popup.
- Offseason uses one selected client and a short stage indicator. Renew representation, choose a career route, compare offers, then commit. Certain renewals show the free current-terms option; contested renewals show alternatives and their consequences.
- School confirmation names the destination, season, cost, projected playing time, payment and commitment lock. After signing, competing school and draft actions disappear. Transfer payment and the following season's school change are separate results.
- Development defaults to media work for an outstanding visibility promise; promises name concrete deliverables rather than only abstract priorities.

## Phone presentation

16px body and controls, 14px secondary text, 48px minimum action targets, compact goal bar, scrollable result sheets and no horizontally clipped navigation. The main career action precedes profile history and explanatory disclosures.

## Scope and remaining design questions

This pass changes presentation and action feedback over the existing simulation. It does not rebalance the economy or establish that the core game is fun. Recurring client requests can still feel repetitive; the number and quality of meaningful tradeoffs need further playtesting. Pro preparation and detailed player records remain relatively dense secondary screens. The paused AAA review skill was not used.
