# Pitch production prototype

Date: September 7, 2026.

Status: historical proposal, on hold. The later discussion moved production after agreement where appropriate, then prioritized talent discovery, recruiting competition, money/prestige progression and player development. See [Agency progression and player development](AGENCY_PROGRESSION_AND_DEVELOPMENT.md) for the current direction. The six buyer categories remain candidates; this document's mandatory pitch-production sequence is not being implemented.

## Repeatable player experience

Pick a client → produce a pitch → approach a buyer → negotiate a deal → fulfill the commitments → get paid.

Pitch production is the equivalent of producing a music demo. The agent buys and coordinates commercial work, then sells the client's appeal. Football results happen in the simulation and change demand; pitch production does not improve football ability.

A buyer can be found proactively or send an incoming opportunity after a recorded performance, change in exposure, or other relevant event. An incoming brief can start the process before production. Both routes use the same builder. Existing suitable pitch material can be reused with an explicit adaptation cost.

## Six commercial opportunities for the test

| Opportunity | What the buyer wants to see | Suitable production approach | Paid commitment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Local restaurant | A recognizable local player who makes a personal connection | Hometown storyteller, restaurant location, personable introduction | Local promotion and an appearance |
| Local car dealership | Local recognition and confident product presentation | Product videographer, showroom, lifestyle spot | Promotional shoot and scheduled appearance |
| National restaurant chain | Broad appeal and a clear, polished campaign | Commercial crew, controlled set, product-focused concept | Campaign content and promotion |
| Video game | A credible connection to football or gaming audiences | Digital-content specialist, gaming setup or football setting, personality-led feature | Fictional game promotion or likeness campaign |
| Sneaker deal | Athletic identity, style, and audience fit | Sports videographer, field or court, action showcase | Branded content and promotional commitments |
| Sports camps | An approachable athlete whom families trust | Community storyteller, practice field, mentoring demonstration | Paid camp appearance or hosting commitment |

These are six commercial deal categories, not a requirement that every client complete all six. Sports camps use the shared deal process, not a separate camp-management game. School/team compensation remains an important earnings source to design separately; these six do not replace it or imply that school negotiations are already implemented.

## Build the pitch

Use one production screen with four concrete choices:

1. **Studio:** production facilities and support, affecting usable picture/audio quality, cost, and turnaround. A simple interview room, product set, and mobile production crew serve different briefs.
2. **Videographer:** specialty and style. Hometown storytelling, product advertising, and sports action offer different strengths rather than a universal best hire.
3. **Location:** the actual setting and its relevance to the buyer. Restaurants, showrooms, fields, gaming rooms, and studio sets have different costs and visual uses.
4. **Concept:** what the client will actually say or do: tell a personal story, present a product, demonstrate athletic identity, or welcome and teach younger players.

Explain each choice in plain language beside its price and duration. Show total cost, completion date, and buyer fit before spending. Reject a production schedule that misses an active brief's deadline before charging. Use a compact finished pitch card showing client, concept, crew, location, and completed material as a durable result.

Production quality, buyer fit, client appeal, asking price, competition, and buyer budget influence the response. Expensive production cannot manufacture national demand for an unknown client. A modest authentic hometown pitch can outperform an expensive generic ad for a local restaurant. Explain the meaningful reasons for an offer or rejection; do not hide the process behind a single unexplained success score.

## Visible results and money

Show a clear sequence: brief → production in progress → finished pitch → buyer response → signed deal → commitments completed → payment.

The buyer response is a dedicated result panel, including the offer, obligations, timing, and reasons. Allow accepting, asking for more money, or proposing fewer obligations with explicit tradeoffs. A rejection should identify an actionable issue without guaranteeing a successful retry.

Keep pitch production and contracted campaign delivery distinct: the first wins the business; the latter fulfills the signed promise. Reuse suitable material where the fictional deal permits it, with any additional cost shown before agreement.

Display client earnings, agency commission, upfront production spending, remaining delivery costs, and agency project profit separately. Label forecasts as estimates until an offer exists. Performance can create future opportunities but does not retroactively change guaranteed signed payments.

Save a successful setup as a named production preset. Reusing it carries over choices, not a guaranteed result; show current prices, availability, and fit for the new client and buyer.

## Initial playtest checks

- A new player can complete a local restaurant project and explain what they bought, why the buyer offered a deal, and what the agency earned.
- All six categories share the same understandable production and deal flow.
- Two different clients can make different production choices rational for the same category.
- A cheaper relevant pitch can beat a more expensive mismatched pitch.
- A performance-driven incoming brief names the recorded event that generated it and enters the same workflow as a proactively found buyer.
- Every paid production leaves an inspectable finished pitch, including after rejection.
- The first deal produces a visible commitment result and correctly reconciled client payment, commission, costs, and project profit.
- Saving and adapting a preset reduces repeated setup without removing buyer-fit decisions.
- Existing depth chart, health, school, conference, personality, scouting, and eligibility information supports commercial judgment without becoming additional mandatory production steps.

## Scope discipline

Test the six buyer categories and one shared production builder before adding more categories, crew management, complex editing, camp operations, or additional progression systems. Retain personality and the earlier CD Market-inspired ideas as future candidates. The core promise is making better commercial deals for clients and seeing the money and results.
