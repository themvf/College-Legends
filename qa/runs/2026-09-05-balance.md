# D3 — `prospectOdds` calibration

**Sample.** 4 leagues × 72 programs × 1 full regular season (weeks 1–14).
Seeds `qa-c3-odds-1` … `qa-c3-odds-4`. **16,680 posted-odds observations**
(target was ≥2,000), of which **4,172 `SIGN`** and 12,508 `HOLD`.
`NOT_PURSUING` rows are excluded at collection, so every row is a program that
was already a contender when the number was posted.

Method (`d3.mjs`): at each week, build `recruitingOddsIndex(state)` once, call
`prospectOdds(state, program, prospect, index)` for every live (program,
prospect) pair, record `percent`/`contested`/`contenders`, then advance the same
state **twice** and record whether the program ended the week holding him:

- **PURE** — `advanceWeek(state, [])`. Nothing at all happens between the number
  being posted and the market resolving.
- **LIVE** — `advanceWeek(state, planWeeklyCommands(state))`. The career then
  continues from LIVE, so the two arms are always off the same state.

## D3.1 Observation — which arm the player sees, and why the arms differ

This was the adjudication the brief asked for, so the reading of the code comes
first and the measurement second.

**When the number is computed, relative to when offers land.**

- `prospectOdds` is a pure read on a `GameState`. It consumes no RNG
  (`index.ts:3393-3412`), and `winProbability` (`index.ts:3506`) is a quadrature
  over the *stored* base scores of `contendingPrograms(state, prospect, index)`
  (`index.ts:3556`).
- The board calls it on `game.state`:
  `recruiting-view-model.ts:259` — `odds: prospectOdds(state, programId, prospect.id, oddsIndex)`.
  The same function's `pending` argument (the player's queued commands) is used
  only to set the `queuedOffer` / `queuedInvestment` flags at lines 229–232. **It
  is not folded into the odds.**
- Recruiting commands are queued, not applied on click (`App.tsx:2038`,
  `Recruiting.tsx:397/418` — "Offer change queued", "+20 RP queued"). They are
  applied inside `advanceWeek`, in the `commands → recruiting market` order, so
  the reader's own offers and every rival's land **after** the number was read
  and **before** the market resolves.
- The one exception is money: `Recruiting.tsx:470` re-prices the row with
  `prospectOdds(..., { nilOffer: amount })` as the slider moves.

So the number the player sees is **the PURE arm's question** — "the chance he
commits to you this week if nobody, including you, spends anything more". The
LIVE arm grades that number against a different question. Both arms are worth
reporting, and neither on its own is the story.

**Which side of the week carries the LIVE gap.** Two follow-up runs, same four
seeds, same 72 programs, same 14 weeks. A designated quarter of each league
(18 programs, `i % 4 === 0`) has its own recruiting commands stripped from the
planner's output while every rival keeps theirs, so for those programs the only
thing that moves between posting and resolution is what rivals did.

`d3b.mjs` / `d3c.mjs`, **n = 878 muted-program `SIGN` rows, 4 leagues:**

| arm | what is restored | observed sign rate |
|---|---|---|
| *posted* | — | **8.1%** |
| `rivals` | nothing of the reader's own | **7.2%** |
| `offer` | own `OFFER_PROSPECT` | 6.9% |
| `points` | own `INVEST_RECRUITING_POINTS` | **20.0%** |
| `nil` | own `SET_NIL_OFFER` | **19.6%** |
| `live` | everything | **31.7%** |

Per seed, `rivals` = 8.5 / 7.2 / 4.9 / 8.1% against posted 8.9 / 7.2 / 7.3 / 9.0%.

**Observation: rival activity inside the week is worth about one point** (8.1
posted → 7.2 delivered). That is the direction and roughly the magnitude the
docstring claims for it — *"a contested recruit's real chance is at or below what
is posted"*. **The whole of the LIVE gap is the reader's own week**, and inside
that it is pursuit points and NIL money in roughly equal share; the bare
scholarship offer is worth nothing on its own (6.9% against 7.2%, inside noise).

**Where it concentrates.** Of the 3,693 rows posted in the 0–9% decile:

| | n | PURE | LIVE |
|---|---|---|---|
| contested at posting | 2,335 | 0.0% | **2.8%** |
| uncontested at posting | 1,358 | 0.1% | **54.3%** |

So the "0–9% posted signs 21.8% of the time" headline is almost entirely rows
where **the reader is the only program chasing him and is below the commitment
threshold**. The card's own sentence for that state says so in words — *"Nobody
has done enough to make him commit yet — he needs N more from somebody"* — while
the number beside it says 0%.

## D3.4 Observation — monotonicity, per arm

The brief singles this out as the result that would be a bug rather than tuning.
**It holds in every arm.**

| arm | deciles with n≥20 | coarse bands (0–9 / 10–29 / 30–49 / 50–69 / 70–100) | point-biserial *r* (posted > 0) |
|---|---|---|---|
| PURE | 2 inversions: 25.0→20.8 and 46.5→45.5 | **monotone** | **0.667** (n=562) |
| LIVE | 2 inversions: 90.7→81.8 and 97.8→90.9 | **monotone** | 0.295 (n=562) |
| `rivals` (D3b) | **monotone** | — | — |

Every decile inversion is ≤4.2 points against Wilson intervals 13–17 points wide
on n = 36–53. There is no monotonicity break to report in either arm; the LIVE
relationship is merely much weaker (*r* 0.295 against 0.667), which is what a
uniform additive lift from a variable the number cannot see looks like.

## D3.5 Observation — a small conservative bias where the code says the number is exact

Secondary, and **inside the brief's band**, so it is an observation rather than a
finding. The docstring says an uncontested row is *exact*, and for an uncontested
row the arithmetic in `winProbability` reduces to exactly what
`resolveRecruitingMarket` applies. Pooled Poisson-binomial test, PURE arm:

| set | n | expected | observed | z |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| all `SIGN` rows | 4,172 | 284.9 | 264 | −2.37 |
| posted > 0, uncontested | 439 | 240.7 | 222 | **−2.38** |
| posted > 0, contested | 123 | 44.2 | 42 | −0.54 |
| posted 30–79%, uncontested | 191 | 102.5 | 86 | **−2.50** |

About four percentage points low on uncontested rows, spread across weeks rather
than concentrated (per-week z ranges −2.66 to +1.27).

**Hypothesis, tested and refuted.** I expected openings exhaustion:
`resolveRecruitingMarket` decrements `openingsByProgram` as contests resolve and
skips a winner whose openings have run out (`index.ts:3642`), while
`prospectOdds` prices every row independently. If that were the cause the
shortfall would grow with the number of wins a program expects in the week. It
does not — `d3-openings.mjs`, 2,513 program-weeks:

| Σ posted p for the program-week | program-weeks | expected | observed | shortfall per expected win |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–0.5 | 2,236 | 56.4 | 48 | **−0.149** |
| 0.5–1 | 204 | 150.9 | 144 | −0.046 |
| 1–2 | 72 | 75.6 | 70 | −0.074 |

Only 9 of 2,513 program-weeks expect more than one win, and the shortfall is
*largest* in the band that expects fewest. **Openings exhaustion is not the
cause and I do not have one.** Worth someone's half hour, not mine on this cycle.

## D3.6 Diagnosis — hypothesis

*Labelled hypothesis. The observations above stand without it.*

The LIVE gap is **not** the class of error the cycle-2 correction recorded in
`CLAUDE.md`. That correction was about a harness advancing with rival commands
and grading the odds against a market rivals had changed; here the rival side is
measured directly and is worth **0.9 points**, which is the documented
upper-bound behaviour working as designed.

What the measurement shows instead is narrower and, I think, still real:
`prospectOdds` answers *"if nothing further is spent"*, the screen does not say
so, and of the three things the reader can spend that week — a scholarship
offer, pursuit points, and NIL money — **only NIL has a preview path**. Pursuit
points move the outcome by the same order as NIL (7.2% → 20.0% against 7.2% →
19.6%, n=878) and move the posted number by exactly nothing, because
`buildProspectBoard` passes `pending` to the flags and not to the odds.

The player-visible consequence is a number that does not respond to the control
the screen puts next to it — which is the same complaint the odds feature was
built to answer (*"A cold player invested 20 points in a prospect showing 0% and
watched nothing move"*). The direction is generous rather than punishing, so I
would not call it a lie; I would call it the pursuit-points half of the feature
not having been finished.

**Not a P1 on my reading**, and I would resist "the odds are wrong by 45 points"
as the statement. The honest one-liner: *the recruiting board's percentage is
correct for the board as it stands and does not move when you invest pursuit
points, which are worth about 13 points of signing probability.*

## D2(f) Observation — **the card posts a week the button cannot produce**

*This is the one I would reproduce first.*

Found by reading `priorities.ts` while building the (b) harness, then measured.
`weekPriorities` projects "make it a priority" as

```
// packages/simulation/src/priorities.ts:420-421
const withFocus = (focus: WeekFocus): WeekHourPlan =>
  planWeekHours(state, programId, chosenSet.has(focus) ? chosen
    : [...chosen.slice(0, Math.max(0, chosen.length - 1)), focus]);
```

It **always drops the last standing priority**. The button the player presses
does something different:

```
// apps/web/src/App.tsx:2483-2488
const next = isChosen(focus)
  ? chosen.filter((entry) => entry !== focus)
  : [...chosen, focus].slice(-capacity.capacity);
```

cards read, then the week advanced with `chosen + [card]`. 4 leagues,
seeds `qa-c3-slot-1` … `-4`.

| INSTALL_OFFENSE | 196 | 73.30 | 73.07 | −0.23 | 2.80 | 33 | 73.26 | −0.03 | **0** |

The right-hand pair is the control that makes this a diagnosis rather than a
guess: **against the plan the card assumed, posted matches delivered on every
single row.** Against the plan the button produces, it does not. The mean bias at
full capacity is under one point and runs in both directions, so this half is a
much smaller matter than (i) — but 167 of 221 RECRUIT rows still miss a ±0.5
tolerance, with a worst case of 4.0 points.

**Diagnosis — hypothesis.** One rule for "what happens if I pick this" is
implemented twice, in the engine's projection and in the UI's toggle, and they
disagree about which standing priority gets displaced. `planWeekHours` is
already the single source of the hours; what is missing is a single source for
*the focus list a click would produce*. Exporting that from `priorities.ts` and
having both `withFocus` and `toggle` call it would close both halves at once.
This is a `CLAUDE.md` "payoffs are visible" breach — *"a card that disagrees with
the engine breaks payoffs-are-visible, which is an invariant rather than a
nicety"* — on the recruiting card, in the state the dashboard actively directs
the player into.

# D1 — pooled competitiveness baseline

**Sample.** Six leagues per size, 72 programs and 24 programs, one full regular
season each, AI planning every week. Seeds `qa-c3-pool-1` … `-6` (72) and
`qa-c3-pool24-1` … `-6` (24). **2,592 + 864 = 3,456 games.**

were originally measured at the size `CLAUDE.md` says the project tunes at
("Tune at 24 programs, ship, re-verify at 72"). Without the bracket I could not
tell a regression from a league-size artifact.

## D1 — against the bands

| figure | §2 band | 72 programs (n=2,592) | 24 programs (n=864) | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| home win rate | 57–60% | **60.1%** | 57.5% | at the top edge; in band at 24, 0.1 over at 72 |
| one-score games | 21.4% (deviation, §6) | 24.2% | 22.9% | **above** the recorded value at both sizes — i.e. better than recorded, not worse |
| average margin | ~15 | **21.5** | **22.4** | **outside, +43% to +49%** |
| shutouts | ~0.2% | **2.62%** | **2.55%** | **outside, ~13x** |
| points / team-game | ~27.8 | 26.5 | 27.0 | in band (−4.7% / −2.9%, tolerance ±8%) |

**Observation.** Average margin and shutout rate are outside their §2 bands, by
a wide margin, at both league sizes, in **12 of 12 independent leagues**, with
per-league sd of 0.55 and 0.42 respectively. Neither is listed in §6.
**League size is not the explanation**: the two sizes agree to within 0.9 margin
and 0.07 percentage points of shutouts.

Nothing since cycle 2 has touched game resolution, so I do not believe this is a
cycle-3 regression, and I want to be clear that I have no earlier balance-tester
run to compare against — the brief says this is the first one.

**Diagnosis — hypothesis, and a caution against over-reading it.** Average
margin, shutout rate and one-score rate are three views of one distribution, and
§6 already owns the third: *"unit ratings are deliberately sensitive… the league
also mixes power and low-tier programs inside divisions"*. A league whose
one-score rate is 24% instead of a real 35% must have a fatter margin than 15 and
more shutouts than 0.2%. `CLAUDE.md` also predicted the direction when the
five-card week shipped — *"execution now varies between programs, which is
correct… and it compounds the existing tier gap. Worth watching."*

So my best reading is that **§2's margin and shutout rows are stale rather than
breached** — they date from the 2026-07 RNG-finalizer table, before unit
resolution, snap weighting and per-program install execution — and that the right
outcome is to re-baseline them or fold them into the §6 entry that already
explains them, rather than to open a tuning task. **That is a hypothesis. The
measurement is that two stated figures are outside their stated bands by 43% and
13x, pooled over twelve leagues and 3,456 games.**
