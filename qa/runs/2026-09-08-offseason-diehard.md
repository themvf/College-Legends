## What was fun

1. **The renewal remembers the relationship.** Delivered service, completed ambition, actual earnings, trust, and a stated desire to continue connect last season's choices to this season's contract. This is a material improvement over repeated generic support requests.
2. **The market expresses football priorities.** Twin Rivers offers an FBS setting but only 12% snaps. Rejecting that badge for a starting role feels like representing my player rather than maximizing a single prestige number.
3. **Negotiation has a specific payoff.** Mountain's $159k became $183k after a 57% counter, with withdrawal risk and a Pine Coast fallback. I then saw the stated commission arrive and the next-season destination actually change.

**Precise desire to continue:** Seeing the market comparison between Mountain's stronger FCS starting job and Twin Rivers' FBS backup role. This is the first offseason decision that made me think about Caleb's final football year as a distinct chapter. The accepted counter and verified Mountain handoff reinforced it.

## What was less fun or unclear

1. **Mountain was close to a dominant choice in this draw.** It offered the highest client net, the best agency margin, a stronger team than Pine Coast, and the same starting snaps. Moving cost was already included and did not overturn the advantage. Staying would mainly be a sentimental choice based on information presented. This is an observation about these three offers, not proof that all markets lack tradeoffs.
2. **Resolved decisions left contradictory or irrelevant text.** A disabled “Ask $211k” suggested a nonexistent next negotiation after the single counter was used. At payout, the old recap still said Caleb was returning to Pine Coast despite the signed Mountain contract. The latter was the most serious clarity problem: it made the destination appear uncertain until handoff.
3. **Calendar language needed a clearer year distinction.** A 2029 school agreement pays in the 2028 offseason at Draft Weekend. The later explicit receipt clarified this. Before that, a future-season contract and current-offseason payout were easy to conflate. The phase timeline itself helped explain the compressed winter/summer and parallel playoff events.

**No quit moment occurred in this follow-up.** The destination contradiction reduced confidence, but I wanted to verify the handoff rather than stop. This does not establish long-term retention.

## Prioritized pointers

1. **Ensure every recap uses the committed destination and guarantee status.** The player card, agreement receipt, payout recap, and handoff must tell the same story. Old Pine Coast text was observed; root reported a fix, but the original payout event was not replayed in this browser session.
2. **Replace exhausted actions with their result.** After one counter, show “Counter used — accepted $183,000” or the actual rejection outcome, not a disabled hypothetical higher ask. Root reported changing this label; I did not receive a fresh counter-screen observation confirming it.
3. **Put payment year and football season beside each other consistently.** The updated receipt was directly relayed as: “Paid during the 2028 offseason, before 2029 season.” Preserve that clarity across offer, signing, and payout screens, along with the distinction between school guarantees and later sponsor campaigns.
4. **Give some otherwise superior transfer offers a visible football or relationship tradeoff.** Possibilities include role uncertainty, scheme fit, adaptation cost, or a concrete continuity benefit at the current school. These are suggestions, not claims that those mechanics are currently absent everywhere. Do not make staying artificially optimal; make its reasons assessable.
5. **Keep the handoff focused on the player's next football question.** Caleb arrives for his final eligibility season as QB1 at a better team. A concise next-year aim tied to that situation would build on the good dispatch and help the user immediately care about the new season. The renewal's evidence-backed personal explanation is the model to preserve.
