## 5. My verdict on the three P1s already fixed

The implementer asked to be triaged alongside the findings. Taking them in
order:

**Recruiting board white-screen — the fix matches the finding.** A `useMemo`
below an early return, moved above it. The regression test clicks every row and
crosses the evaluated/unevaluated branch, and was confirmed red against the
broken build; the note that its own first draft passed vacuously is the right
instinct and is the reason I re-verified it in a real career rather than a
fixture. I bought one evaluation, advanced a week so it committed, confirmed
the board then held a mixed state, and clicked all ten rows: no unmount, no
console error. **Verified.** One thing the fix does not do, and does not claim
to: the reason this was a white screen rather than a broken panel is that the
app has no error boundary at all. Filed as issue 13 so the class is not closed
along with the instance.

**The weekly card — the fix is correct and closes half the finding.** The
recruiting formula is now shared and I measured the card against the engine on
four programs in two focus states: eight of eight exact. But F3 was filed with
*two* tables, and the commit message addresses only one of them. The scouting
half — `+2.9 → +2.5` in week 1 and `+2.1 → +1.5` in every week from 2 to 8, the
larger sample and the larger error — is untouched and reproduces on demand. It
is now issue 02, still P1. This is the clearest argument for the process change
that produced this report: a triaged issue list would have split F3 into two
issues with two severities, and half of a P1 would not have been closed by a
commit that never mentioned it.

**Preseason ticket price — the dismissal is correct, and I am upholding it on
my own evidence rather than deferring.** I drove the app to the preseason
Business tab and tried to move the slider four ways a player can:
`click` → timeout, `fill` → timeout, keyboard `ArrowRight` after `focus` → value
unchanged at 42. The panel is a `locked-panel` carrying *"There's no game this
week, so there's no gate. Pricing applies to your next home game."* In
`ROSTER_REVIEW` the week is 0, so no fixture is found, so `atHome` is false and
the input is `disabled` — and it was `disabled` at `084652b` too, which is the
build the reporter ran. A synthetic `input` event *does* queue the command; the
header then reads `1 preseason decision queued`. That is what a driver can do
and a player cannot.

The reporter's own screenshot settles it: `19-ticket-48.png` shows `$48 a seat`
sitting directly beneath the locked note, on a greyed panel. The control was
never live. The reporter's arithmetic ($2.1M ÷ 50,000 = $42) is correct and
proves the price never changed — which is the engine refusing the command, not
losing it.

The real defect underneath was the silence, and it was real: before `ad06e2f` a
`COMMAND_REJECTED` reached only the inbox feed unless it came from an audited
weekly-planning command. That is a §8 breach and it is fixed — I confirmed the
reason now renders on screen: *"Only sponsorship, depth-chart, redshirt, and
preseason scheduling decisions can be made before the season begins."*

**Calibration for the tester, recorded because rejections are the signal:** a
control moved by a synthetic event is not a player action. Report the click
path you used and whether the element was enabled, and if a real click fails,
that *is* the finding.

## 7. The claim under test: is anything spanning more than a week legible now?

Cycle 1 closed on one sentence — *"The game taught me the week and never taught
me the program"* — and the disposition set a test: a second cold read should be
able to answer, unaided, how many players leave each year, what last week's
money went on, whether the job is safe, and what switching scheme costs.

The reporter's own verdict was "partly". **Mine is: the claim is two-thirds
made, and the third that failed failed for a reason nobody predicted.**

**Roster churn and the ledger are done.** Not "improved" — done. The reporter
understood the eligibility cliff in week 0 from the dashboard alone, had it
repeated at position level in the War Room, and saw it again as portal
openings; and *Where last week went* itemises every cost with its own basis and
reconciles to the week's net. Both were the cycle-1 complaints verbatim and
both are now cited as the best screens in the game. I would leave them alone.

**Job security is legible, with a gap that is smaller than it looks.** The band
shows from a meaningful record, it grades pace rather than partial record, the
February verdict itemises and sums, and it grades the same target the header
stated in week 1. The reporter could answer "is my job safe" and "what is the
board measuring" without help. What is missing is only the scale — `96 from 65`
on an unnamed range — which is a sentence of copy, not a design gap.

**Scheme is not legible, and the reason is not the copy.** The cycle-1 fix
posted the install number per option, which was the smaller half. The bigger
half is that `rosterSchemeFit` spreads the five options *against each other*
and centres them on 64, so the percentage is a ranking rendered as an absolute
— and the screen invites the player to compare this year's number with last
year's, which is the one comparison it cannot support. That is why "my fit
collapsed 25 points and nothing says why" is unanswerable: nothing *did*
happen to the roster in proportion to the number that moved. Adding copy to
this screen will not fix it. Issue 03.

**Recruiting is not legible and could not be tested.** The evaluation half is
genuinely good now — `Overall Unknown → 51–58`, `Ask/week → $350–$550`, and the
line *"he needs 6 more from somebody"*, which is the single best sentence in
the system. Everything after the evaluation is invisible: pursuit posts no
odds, searches report nothing, free offers report nothing and produced four of
five signings, and the one commitment the reporter earned was announced only in
an Inbox that is broken. And the board crashed, so the loop could not be run at
all. This is the largest untested area in the project, and it is now also the
most recently rewritten one. See §9.

## 8. The two structural questions

**Does the weekly loop solve itself in week 1?** On its own posted numbers,
yes — and I measured it on a second seed and program. One slot is worth +10 to
+12 points of execution on an install card, +0.3 readiness on the scout card,
and +6 of 44 points on the trail card. A capacity-2 program has three cards
worth taking and two that can never win.

But I am **not** filing that as a defect in the design, because standing
priorities are deliberate and correct: *"nobody plays season twelve if the week
costs real attention every time."* What the design depends on is the re-prompt
— the briefing flagging a card worth ≥65 that nobody is chasing — and in
eighteen weeks of play it never fired usefully. So the honest reading is that
the architecture is sound and two of its five inputs are mispriced, plus one
genuine defect underneath: the scout card posts numbers the engine does not
deliver *and* the delivered value is identical whether the slot is spent or
not, so that card is not a decision at all (issue 02). Filed as issue 12 at P3
with the balance question marked as needing a proper sample, because
`CLAUDE.md` already records the stakes coefficients as untuned hypotheses and
one program is not a measurement.

**Is the buried Development screen a defect or an architecture complaint?** A
defect, P2, and worse than the report makes it sound. If it were only that the
screen sits at item 4 of 10 behind `More ▾`, it would be P3 discoverability.
But all three routes that point at development lead to a lesser picker whose
three call sites hard-code `focus: "TECHNIQUE"`. So the player is not merely
failing to find a decision — the game is making a four-way choice on their
behalf, every week, for a season, on the system `CLAUDE.md` measured at 2.74
Overall against 1.29 and deliberately re-tuned `SPOTLIGHT_INTENSITY` to make
worth taking. Conditioning also moves this week's injury risk by 15% in either
direction, so the silent default has a health consequence too. Issue 07.

## 11. Recommendation

**Fix first. Do not ship to testers.**

The bar is not "does it crash" — it did not crash once in twelve headless
seasons and the reporter completed a season, an offseason and part of another
without losing a career. The bar is whether a tester's report would be about
the game. Right now it would be about the Inbox, the depth chart's starter
counts, and a recruiting screen whose payoffs are invisible, and those reports
would tell us nothing we do not already know.

Order, and the reasoning:

1. **Issue 02**, the open P1. An invariant, a one-place fix, and it is on the
   card that prices a weekly decision.
2. **Issues 04, 05, 07** — the three P2s that are each a small, located change
   with an outsized effect on what a player understands. 04 is a constant, 05 is
   a filter and a fallback string, 07 is a route and a hard-coded enum. All
   three have been reported before in some form.
3. **Issues 06, 08, 09** — the three P2s that need a screen built or a flow
   rerouted. More work, and each removes a specific "I had to guess".
4. **Issue 03**, which is design work rather than a fix and should be scoped
   before it is started.
5. The P3s and the clarity cluster with whatever pass touches those screens.

Two process points worth carrying:

- Issue 13 first if anything else on the web app is being touched. An error
  boundary is what decides the severity of the *next* render bug, and this
  cycle spent a P1 on that lesson.
- Both invariant breaches this cycle were **posted numbers disagreeing with the
  engine**, and the same class produced two of cycle 1's four. That is now
  three of six defects at P1/P2 across two cycles from one root cause: a screen
  carrying its own copy of an engine formula. It is worth a standing check —
  every projection function called by the UI, asserted against the event the
  week emits — rather than being found one card at a time.
