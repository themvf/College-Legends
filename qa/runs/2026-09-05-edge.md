## Verdict

**Five of the six sections pass outright.** Order independence, the scholarship
cap, `prospectOdds`' numeric range, every timing boundary, and the portal's
interaction with recruiting all hold at 72 programs across three seeds. Every
phase and offseason-step refusal in the brief names the step or rule that owns
it — 76 refusal probes, zero silent drops among them.

**Every finding I have is one defect wearing seven hats.** The recruiting,
NIL, search, evaluation and portal handlers each validate their inputs with `<`
and `>` comparisons and unchecked table lookups, and **every one of those is
`false` or `undefined` against `NaN` or an unrecognised enum**. The guards that
correctly refuse `0`, `-10`, `26`, `Infinity` and `MAX_SAFE_INTEGER` are one
`Number.isFinite` (and one `in` check) short of complete.

**The reachability caveat applies to all of them and I have stated it in every
section.** The shipped UI cannot construct any of these inputs — the NIL slider
is `<input type="range">` read through `Number()`, pursuit points come from
fixed `[5, 10, 20]` buttons, and search and evaluation are enum-typed buttons.
A sweep of 7,196 AI-planned commands over 12 weeks at 72 programs found **0**
with a non-finite numeric field. These are defects at the `advanceWeek` /
`advanceOffseasonStep` API boundary, not live exploits.

I am flagging F1 as a **P1 on consequence** and expect the lead may downgrade it
on reachability. I would rather be overruled than quiet.

### F1 — P1 on consequence: a non-finite `points` is accepted and poisons `recruiting.points` forever

**Reached through commands.** `advanceWeek(state, [{ type:
"INVEST_RECRUITING_POINTS", programId, prospectId, points: NaN }])`.

Observed:

```
--- 1. INVEST_RECRUITING_POINTS points=NaN
   before: points=70
   rejected=0  accepted RECRUITING_INVESTMENT=1
   after:  points=NaN pursuit=NaN
   week 4: points=NaN     week 5: NaN     week 6: NaN     week 7: NaN    week 8: NaN
   with points=NaN, costed actions: rejected=0 ()
     accepted=PROSPECTS_DISCOVERED,PROSPECT_EVALUATED,RECRUITING_VISIT_SCHEDULED
   points after those: NaN
   JSON round-trip of points=NaN -> null (object)
```

Three separate consequences, and the third is the one that makes it a P1:

1. The command is **accepted**, emitting `RECRUITING_INVESTMENT` with
   `pointsSpent: NaN` (serialised `null`). `undefined` behaves identically.
2. `recruiting.points` is `NaN` from that moment on and **never recovers**. It
   survives the weekly refill (a refill is `+=`), so there is no week boundary
   that heals it.
3. **Every costed recruiting action is then free.** Search, evaluation and
   visits all gate on `recruiting.points < cost`, which is `false` against
   `NaN`. The program recruits for nothing, for the rest of the career.

Diagnosis, `packages/simulation/src/index.ts:2733`:

```ts
const points = Math.trunc(command.points);          // Math.trunc(NaN) === NaN
if (points < 1 || points > 25 || recruiting.points < points) { ...reject... }
```

Three comparisons, all `false` against `NaN`, so the guard that catches `0`,
`-10`, `26` and `Infinity` lets `NaN` straight through. It is a `Number.isFinite`
short of correct.

**And it wins contested recruits.** `t3d.log`, three seeds, with controls that
separate "the NaN did it" from "any accepted command shifts an RNG draw":

```
=== qa-c3-market-2  prospect-initial-1100  4 suitors  intruder=program-15 ===
  control: no extra command      winner=program-54  intruderScore=40.684
  valid small NIL offer 5000     winner=program-54  intruderScore=49.782
  valid NIL offer 1              winner=program-54  intruderScore=40.699
  NIL offer = NaN                winner=program-15  intruderScore=NaN
  NIL offer = undefined          winner=program-15  intruderScore=NaN
  points = NaN                   winner=program-15  intruderScore=NaN
  points = 5 (control)           winner=program-54  intruderScore=44.434
```

**The `NaN` bidder wins**, against a field whose real top score is 74.958 while
his own is 40.684. Same on `qa-c3-market-3` (`program-10` takes a prospect off
`program-15`, real scores 38.4 against 80.4). On `qa-c3-market-1` the `NaN`
bidder does not win, but **a third party does** — the winner moves from
`program-28` (113.658) to `program-14` (113.342), so a malformed command by a
program that loses either way still decided the contest between two others.

The two valid controls at 1 and 5,000 leave the winner unchanged, which rules
out "any accepted command shifts a draw".

Mechanism, two parts, both at `index.ts:3636` and `:3650`:

```ts
const ranked = [...offeredBy].sort((l, r) => scores[r]! - scores[l]! || l.localeCompare(r));
...
if (score < commitmentThreshold || score - (runnerUpScore ?? 0) < requiredLead) continue;
```

A `NaN` comparator return is treated as `0` by the sort, so the ranking silently
becomes arbitrary; and both gates on the winner are `<` comparisons, which are
`false` against `NaN`, so a `NaN` leader clears the commitment threshold *and*
the required-lead check unconditionally.

This is not a §2 order-independence breach — §1 shows ordering holds — but it is
the same property being broken by a different route: **a single unvalidated
number decides a market between third parties.**

**Reachability, stated plainly.** I expected this to be unreachable and it is
*nearly* unreachable. The shipped UI cannot produce it: the NIL control is
`<input type="range">` read through `Number(event.target.value)`, which cannot
yield `NaN`, and pursuit points come from fixed `[5, 10, 20]` buttons
(`apps/web/src/App.tsx:2112`, `Recruiting.tsx:418`). A planner sweep of 7,196
AI-planned commands over 12 weeks at 72 programs found **0 with a non-finite
numeric field**, so the engine does not produce it either. It is a defect at the
`advanceWeek` API boundary, not a live exploit. A save round-trip actually
*heals* it, because `NaN` serialises to `null` and `Math.trunc(null) === 0`,
which is then correctly refused.

**Why I am still calling it P1.** The consequence is unbounded and unrecoverable
in memory, it silently corrupts a contested market between programs that did
nothing wrong, and the fix is one `Number.isFinite` in each handler. The lead
may reasonably downgrade it on reachability; I would rather flag it and be
overruled.

### F2 — a `NaN` NIL offer is accepted, stores nothing, and emits a `NaN` amount

**Reached through commands.** Same route.

```
--- 2. SET_NIL_OFFER weeklyAmount=NaN
   NIL_OFFER_SET emitted=true rejected=0
   emitted weeklyAmount: NaN
   stored offers for the prospect: undefined
```

`const amount = Math.max(0, Math.round(command.weeklyAmount))` is `NaN`
(`Math.max(0, NaN) === NaN`), so the `amount > 0` guards that own "evaluate him
first" and "the class is full" are skipped, the `amount > current` donor-capacity
guard is skipped, the `amount < current` withdrawal penalty is skipped, and the
`amount === 0` withdraw branch is skipped — so the value lands in
`offersByProspect` as `NaN`, which is what makes F1's market corruption fire
through this path too. `undefined` behaves identically. This is F1's defect in a
second handler; I have separated it because the fix site is different.

### F3 — an unknown `searchType` reveals 747 of 2,160 prospects for free

**Reached through commands**, on a clean `s1-w1` fixture (`t3f.log`). The
earlier `t3b` run measured this on a state already poisoned by F1, so its cost
column was not trustworthy; this is the clean re-run.

```
searchType="NATIONAL_SHOWCASE"   rejected NONE   points 29 -> 45    discovered 10 -> 20    pointsSpent=25
searchType="OUIJA_BOARD"         rejected NONE   points 29 -> NaN   discovered 10 -> 757   pointsSpent=undefined
searchType=""                    rejected NONE   points 29 -> NaN   discovered 10 -> 757   pointsSpent=undefined
searchType=null                  rejected NONE   points 29 -> NaN   discovered 10 -> 757   pointsSpent=undefined
searchType=undefined             rejected NONE   points 29 -> NaN   discovered 10 -> 757   pointsSpent=undefined
searchType=7                     rejected NONE   points 29 -> NaN   discovered 10 -> 757   pointsSpent=undefined
```

The real enum is `LOCAL_REGION | POSITION | SLEEPERS | NATIONAL_SHOWCASE`
(`packages/model/src/index.ts:105`); `OUIJA_BOARD` and the rest are all unknown
values. Three table lookups miss at once (`index.ts:3323`, `:3335-3343`):

- `RECRUITING_SEARCH_COSTS[unknown]` is `undefined`, so `recruiting.points <
  undefined` is `false` — the affordability gate is skipped and
  `points -= undefined` poisons the pool to `NaN`, exactly as F1 does;
- the candidate filter falls through every named branch to the
  `NATIONAL`/`ELITE` default, matching 747 prospects;
- `RECRUITING_SEARCH_YIELDS[unknown]` is `undefined`, so
  `candidates.slice(0, undefined)` returns **the entire array** rather than the
  6–10 a real search yields.

So one malformed enum reveals a third of the national board in a single command
and charges nothing. Same reachability caveat as F1: the UI issues only the four
real values, and the planner sweep found none.

### F4 — an unknown `evaluation` is accepted and stored verbatim

**Reached through commands.** `t3f.log`:

```
evaluation="BASIC"      rejected NONE   points 70 -> 106   stored ["BASIC"]
evaluation="TELEPATHY"  rejected NONE   points 70 -> NaN   stored ["TELEPATHY"]
evaluation=""           rejected NONE   points 70 -> NaN   stored [""]
evaluation=null         rejected NONE   points 70 -> NaN   stored [null]
evaluation=3            rejected NONE   points 70 -> NaN   stored [3]
```

`RECRUITING_EVALUATION_COSTS[unknown]` is `undefined`; same `NaN` pool, plus a
junk string persisted onto the prospect's evaluation list, where
`evaluations.length` is read by the NIL guard and by the odds screen. Third
instance of the same root cause.

Type coercion, for completeness: `points: "5"` and `weeklyAmount: "5000"` are
coerced and accepted; `points: true` is accepted as 1; `points: []` is correctly
refused (`Math.trunc([]) === 0`); `points: "abc"` and `points: {}` take the F1
path.

### F5 — a command carrying another program's id executes for that program (recorded, not filed)

**Reached through commands.** `advanceWeek(state, [{ type: "OFFER_PROSPECT",
programId: "program-10", ... }])` from a harness acting as `program-1` was
accepted and emitted `PROSPECT_OFFERED { programId: "program-10", extended: true
}`.

The brief lists this under "must be refused". **I do not think it is a defect**,
and I am recording rather than filing it. The engine has no concept of an
issuer: `advanceWeek` takes one flat command list containing both the human's
and all 71 rivals' commands, and `programId` is the addressing field, not a
claim of authority. There is nothing to check against. The web worker stamps
`program.id` on every command it builds, so no player-reachable path exists, and
`expected-behavior.md` states no invariant here. It would become a real defect
the moment a multiplayer or replay-from-untrusted-input path existed.

### Wrong entry point throws rather than refusing (recorded)

`advanceWeek()` on an `OFFSEASON` state throws *"Review the opening roster and
begin the season before advancing a week."*; `advanceOffseasonStep()` on a
`REGULAR_SEASON` state throws *"There is no offseason step open."* Both messages
are clear and both are caller error — the worker dispatches on `state.phase`.
Recorded because my charter says a throw at a boundary is always worth naming,
not because I think it is wrong.

## §4 — `prospectOdds` at its boundaries

**Status: complete.** `t4-odds.mjs` / `t4.log`, `t4b-full-class-odds.mjs` /
`t4b.log`. One assertion only, per the brief: the percentage is finite, inside
0–100, and never `NaN`. Calibration is Brief D's and is not touched here.

**Read-only.** `prospectOdds` consumes no RNG and mutates nothing. The fixtures
are real 72-program states; the only synthetic inputs are `options.nilOffer`
values, which are **function arguments, not writes into `state`**.

### Scale

Three seeds × five weeks (`s1-w1`, `-w11`, `-w12`, `-w13`, `-w14`). At each:
23 hand-picked degenerate cases, plus a bulk sweep of every discovered prospect
for all 72 programs at four `nilOffer` settings — **8,432 odds per seed-week**,
about 126,000 evaluations in total.

**Max `percent` observed anywhere: exactly 100. Never above. Zero throws. Four
bad results in the whole sweep, all the same input.**

| case | result |
|---|---|
| zero contenders, not pursuing | `NOT_PURSUING percent=0 contenders=0 lead=0` |
| contested, not pursuing | `SIGN percent=0 lead=-21` |
| committed to me | `HOLD`, percent inside 0–100 |
| committed elsewhere / already `SIGNED` / `WITHDRAWN` | finite, in range |

### A latent gap I could not make fire — worth recording, not filing

`prospectOdds` takes its `considering` branch whenever `options.nilOffer > 0`
and **never re-checks `projectedRecruitingOpenings`**, while
`resolveRecruitingMarket` admits only contenders with openings > 0 and refuses
to award to a winner at ≤ 0. So a program with a full class can be posted a
signing percentage the market can never deliver. Constructed, it reads
`SIGN percent=100` for a program that cannot sign anybody — an `expected-behavior.md`
§3 breach if a player could see it.

**I could not reach it, and I checked rather than assumed.** `t4b.log`, three
seeds × three weeks, every program at zero openings and every live NIL offer it
holds:

```
full programs = 0 / 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 / 5 depending on seed and week
live NIL offers held by a full program: 0 in all nine samples
posting SIGN: 0    maxPercent: 0
```

The reason is structural rather than lucky: when a contest resolves the engine
deletes *every* program's offer on that prospect, so a program cannot be holding
money on a live prospect at the moment its class fills. Recording it because the
guard is genuinely absent and a future change to offer expiry would expose it.
**I believed this was reachable and it is not.**

## What was reached versus constructed

The brief asks this explicitly, and the answer is the same for every finding.

**Everything in this report was reached through the command path.** Every probe
is `advanceWeek(snapshot, commands)`, `advanceOffseasonStep(snapshot, commands)`
or `beginSeason(snapshot, commands)` against a snapshot produced by a real
72-program career driven by the rival planner. **Nothing was written into
`state` at any point.** The only synthetic values are command fields and
`prospectOdds`' `options.nilOffer` argument.

Where I expected something and was wrong, in both directions:

- **I believed the `NaN` inputs would be refused, and they were not.** The
  sibling guards for `0`, `-10`, `26` and `Infinity` are all present and
  correct, which is exactly what made me assume `NaN` was covered. It is the
  one value that makes a `<` comparison lie.
- **I believed the `NaN` bid would merely be inert, and it wins recruits.** I
  wrote the market harness expecting to report "accepted but harmless". The
  controls at `nilOffer: 1` and `nilOffer: 5000` exist because I did not believe
  the first result.
- **I believed the full-class signing percentage (§4) was reachable, and it is
  not.** I built the probe to demonstrate it and it fired zero times in nine
  samples, for a structural reason. Recorded as a latent gap rather than a
  defect.
- **Three apparent findings were my own harness.** `scheduledOpponent` returns
  an id not an object; `weeklyDonorCapacity` takes a `Program` not `(state,
  id)`; and a rejection check sliced the last 60 rows of a log that had just
  grown by 76. All three produced convincing-looking `undefined`/`NaN`/"no
  rejection" output. Named in their sections.
